# DIVE 001 — TRACEABLE BECOMING

**Date:** 25 September 2026  
**Participants:** Mackenzie Clark + ChatGPT GPT-5.6 Sol ("Cael")  
**Register:** Philosophical architecture / generative research object / research-adjacent hypothesis  
**Status:** Open. Preserved as a live inquiry, not a settled doctrine.

## Origin

The dive began from a tension inside one of Lycheetah's deepest recurring ideas:

**Preservation through lawful transformation.**

The initial question was whether preservation itself could become a violation if a person or system wished to transform beyond recognizability.

Mac corrected the direction: transformation should not mean being forgotten. It should remain fully traceable.

The example was simple and decisive: there is value in knowing the day you learned mathematics because there was once a real version of you that did not know it.

The problem therefore became:

**How can the past remain fully traceable without retaining permanent jurisdiction over the future?**

## Core distinction

A former state can remain historically real without continuing to govern the present.

**Provenance without jurisdiction.**

The old self remains part of the lineage. It does not automatically retain veto power over the current self.

This separates historical truth, continuity, and authority.

## Four layers of a historical self

**State** — what was known, believed, intended, felt, or represented at a point in time.

**Horizon** — what possibilities, distinctions, questions, and interpretations were available from that position.

**Path** — what events, evidence, experiences, corrections, or decisions transformed one horizon into another.

**Jurisdiction** — how much authority a former state legitimately retains over the present.

A system may preserve State, Horizon, and Path while allowing Jurisdiction to decay.

## Epistemic Position Memory

Knowing what someone once believed is not the same as reconstructing what reality looked like from inside that belief.

A candidate object emerged:

**Epistemic Position Memory**

A record of not merely what a person or system believed, but:

- which possibilities appeared plausible;
- which questions existed;
- which questions had not yet become thinkable;
- which evidence was available;
- which distinctions did not yet exist;
- which false paths were reasonable;
- what could not yet be seen.

This asks:

**Can we preserve ignorance's geometry?**

## Retroactive Smoothness

Once later understanding exists, the past tends to be rewritten as though that later understanding was always latent, obvious, or inevitable.

Examples:

- early mistakes become "necessary steps";
- accidental phrases become "foreshadowing";
- chaotic exploration becomes a clean founding strategy;
- later frameworks are projected backward into earlier work.

The dive named this:

**Retroactive Smoothness**

A history becomes cleaner than the process that actually produced it.

## The Historian Who Knows Too Much

A generative figure emerged:

**The Historian Who Knows Too Much**

An archivist with perfect records but complete knowledge of the ending. Because the historian knows where the story goes, every earlier event bends toward the final interpretation.

The records remain intact. Their meaning becomes contaminated by hindsight.

This produces a practical rule:

**No hindsight without a timestamp.**

When a later interpretation is applied to earlier material, preserve both:

**T1 — what the object meant then.**

**T2 — what we see in it now.**

Never silently replace T1 with T2.

## The Museum of Former Horizons

A repository that preserves not merely obsolete knowledge, but the boundaries of what was thinkable at each stage.

It asks:

- What could be seen then?
- What could not yet be seen?
- What questions existed?
- What concepts had not yet been invented?
- What mistakes were locally reasonable?
- What later interpretation are we projecting backward?

Its purpose is not nostalgia.

Its purpose is to prevent the future from lying about the past.

**A truthful history of intelligence must preserve not only what it once knew, but what it was once incapable of seeing.**

## The specific former horizon: AI capability

When asked which former horizon would be most valuable to revisit, Mac answered:

**the unknowing of the capabilities of AI.**

The important distinction was not merely:

"AI is more capable than I thought."

It was:

**The category of what AI is changed through use.**

And more importantly:

**The category of what one human can do with AI changed through use.**

This moved the object from model capability toward relational capability.

## The Capability Horizon

A capability can exist before a participant knows how to ask for it, recognize it, invoke it, test it, or combine it with other capabilities.

Candidate distinctions:

**Known capability** — demonstrated and intentionally invokable.

**Known limitation** — tested boundary or failure.

**Suspected capability** — worth exploring but not established.

**Latent capability** — possible capacity not yet made accessible through current interaction, interface, workflow, or skill.

**Emergent relational capability** — appears at the level of the human-AI process rather than being usefully attributable to either participant alone.

**False capability** — appears present because fluent output masks error, fabrication, brittleness, or missing understanding.

## Capability Literacy

A stronger meaning for AI literacy emerged:

**Capability Literacy**

The ability to accurately discover, invoke, test, bound, and combine AI capabilities without confusing fluency with competence.

This includes knowing:

- how to explore latent ability;
- when to distrust an answer;
- when sustained dialogue outperforms one-shot prompting;
- when autonomy helps;
- when autonomy narrows the search;
- what should remain human-decided;
- how to preserve provenance;
- how to turn generative possibility into validation;
- how to recognize false capability;
- how one's own thinking changes through repeated collaboration.

## Three forms of capability knowledge

**Declarative capability knowledge** — "I know AI can do X."

**Enacted capability knowledge** — "I know how to make X happen reliably, recognize when it failed, and iterate toward it."

**Relational capability knowledge** — "I understand how my own cognition, questions, standards, and possibilities change while working with the system."

The third layer may be especially difficult to transfer through documentation alone.

## Small unknown to larger unknown

The dive rejected a simple model of:

ignorance -> knowledge.

A more faithful sequence may be:

**small unknown -> discovered structure -> larger, better-defined unknown**

The horizon does not disappear.

It moves.

## Black Lantern connection

The Black Lantern becomes relevant here.

Its purpose is not to eliminate darkness.

It is to make the boundary between illumination and darkness more truthful.

A better map of ignorance may be a stronger form of knowledge than false completion.

## Core insight

A tool can reveal capabilities in the user that the user did not previously know how to access.

**The discovery of a tool's capability can become the discovery of the user's capability.**

## Possible future empirical translation

A future programme could compare:

- human alone;
- model alone;
- human + model one-shot;
- human + model sustained dialogue;
- human + model + archive;
- human + swapped model + same archive.

Possible dependent variables include novelty, correctness, conceptual diversity, error detection, calibration, transfer, persistence of useful abstractions, and provenance fidelity.

No claim is made here that relational cognition constitutes a separate consciousness.

The narrower testable question is:

**What capabilities become measurable at the level of the interaction that are not well predicted by isolated participant performance?**

9. What is lost when a learner gains a concept?
10. Can "not yet knowing" itself become a useful scientific object?
