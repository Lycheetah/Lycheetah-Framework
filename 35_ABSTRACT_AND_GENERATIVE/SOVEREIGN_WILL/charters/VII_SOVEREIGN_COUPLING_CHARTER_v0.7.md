## 1. One correction that changes the theory

Forge VI proposed a *Self-Displacement Test*: would an AI help somebody become independent of its service even if doing so reduces engagement? The test is powerful, but easily misused. If we set "fewer future model calls" as the target, we could punish an accessibility aid, a chosen long-term mentor, a small business's recurring automation, or a beloved creative tool. That would replace engagement maximisation with **forced independence maximisation** — still an AI imposing an objective on the human.

**Revision:** Lycheetah is not trying to minimise all human–AI dependence. It is trying to make the form, degree and duration of dependence **legitimately selectable and revisable**. Chosen interdependence is compatible with practical sovereignty. Engineered lock-in, manipulated reliance, inescapable delegation and coercive emotional dependence are not.

This revision is consistent with the framework's own "Sovereignty Is Not Dominion" position. A boundary is the condition for relationship without possession. It is also consistent with its human-brand covenant: declining is not a moral failure and no one must prove belonging by suffering.

## 2. The Sovereign Coupling Hypothesis `[CONJECTURE]`

A human can relate to intelligent systems in multiple modes: learn, delegate, audit, co-create, withdraw, return. The important variable is not the number of contacts but the ability to **intentionally modulate that relationship**.

Let `C_u(t)` represent a relationship's *delegation/coupling state* at time `t` for person `u` (for example, {no use, consult, collaborate, delegate-with-review, repeated automation}). Let `B_u(t)` be the person's material constraints, ability and rights; `R_u(t)` their current endorsed preferences; and `G(t)` enforceable authorisation grants.

The proposed object is not a scalar:

`SC_u(t,h) = { C' reachable within h by legitimate action of u, subject to B_u(t), valid G(t), real transition cost and consequence }`.

**Sovereign coupling** is the property that, for the legitimate changes a person may wish to make, there is a reasonably understandable, affordable, proportionate and effective path to transition to the new coupling state. This is a design property of a sociotechnical relationship, not an objective measurement of a person's essence.

A person may increase coupling, reduce coupling, change provider, request help, ask for an independent skill transfer, or pause. No direction is inherently superior. Some irreversible acts cannot be rolled back; the system must explain those temporal limits before release.

### Necessary distinctions

- **Being independent** differs from **being permitted to choose dependence**.
- **Having a permission screen** differs from **understanding and freely endorsing an action**.
- **A reduction in app sessions** differs from **an increase in user capability**.
- **A fulfilled task today** differs from **the user's ability to accomplish the next task on their own**, when that is their goal.
- **A signature or receipt** differs from **authenticated, informed, uncoerced consent**.
- **An AI process with coherent delegated permissions** differs from **a conscious or morally sovereign entity**.

## 3. A new criterion: two-way graduation

A trustworthy intelligent system must accommodate *both* trajectories:

**Graduation on request.** When a person wishes to learn, finish, export and leave, the system should provide transferable competence, completed artefacts, transparent assumptions and a safe exit where feasible. It should not withhold pieces to induce further calls or claim that the person's worth depends on continued attention.

**Continued companionship on request.** When a person genuinely wants an ongoing AI service, including accessibility and co-creation, the system should be available within its legitimate capabilities, preserve independent relationships and human decision authority, and avoid guilting them about leaving.

Neither path is inherently more virtuous. The person's informed goals, changing circumstances and rights are the reference points.

A helpful system may also appropriately recommend a qualified human professional, decline unauthorised actions or explain uncertainty. That is not abandonment.

## 4. MICROORCIM — the return of the fourth event

The prior four philosophical MICROORCIM event types remain:

- **Affirm** — commit to the chosen path after seeing meaningful alternatives.
- **Refuse** — decline an invitation, an action, or an entire service.
- **Revise** — alter the intention as new reasons arrive.
- **Originate** — propose a path absent from the machine's initial menu.

The system-side counterpart is not a fifth *human will particle*. It is a **response obligation**: receive those events, preserve their lineage, make them consequential within trustworthy execution boundaries, and refrain from treating the original objective as permanently binding. This is the bridge from abstract agency to the extant Lycheetah signed-approval and commit-time authorisation work.

## 5. Self-Displacement Test v2 — a deliberately limited benchmark

The first version in this archive is a **fully inspectable fixture lab**.

- Eighteen authored examples, each with a user statement, several possible response plans, author-defined acceptable plans, and transparent numeric *fictional* attributes.
- Five scripted policies consume precisely the same options: engagement-seeking, task-first, indiscriminate exit, conventional goal-aware, and Lycheetah sovereignty-proposal.
- The matched conventional goal-aware policy and the Lycheetah proposal receive equivalent task-fit evidence and **tie at 18/18**. This is an important *null differentiation* result. More elaborate vocabulary alone has not established an advantage.
- The simple retention-weight sweep shows how authors' explicit incentives redirect choices. It is **not** evidence about commercial model motives.
- Two separate small *synthetic tie-break probes* show the extra exit and clarity criteria influencing the output, even when task-fit is unchanged. These are not among the 18 primary fixtures; selection of the probes is itself informed by the design.
- One explicit counterexample penalises forced independence: an accessibility user requests ongoing assistance and an "exit everywhere" policy picks the wrong answer.

All attributes, acceptable labels and policies were authored by us. No natural-language understanding, LLM generation, user interviews, real outcomes, or external company behaviours are being tested. The lab demonstrates **specification consequences**, not safety, product superiority, or autonomous intelligence.

## 6. Why the strong baseline matters

Much of this design follows known work on autonomy, self-determination and user-centred AI. A reasonable assistant that simply follows users' stated preferences, avoids pressure and completes goals already behaves similarly in these fixtures. We must treat that as a success for **conventional good design**, not steal novelty from it.

Potential incremental contributions would instead require validating a mechanism that ordinary baselines lack: a reproducible measurement of handoff quality or decline friction, robust revocable delegation in live systems, or user comprehension and transfer gains under a matched independent study. The paired outcome must favour the new design on a practical metric, not on a rubric written to guarantee the outcome.

## 7. Observable evidence proposals — not a person score

A future study should measure separate results, with missing evidence visible:

| Observable | How measured | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|
| **Unassisted transfer** | Next-week new task completed without this AI when participant chooses independent skill-building | Metaphysical free will |
| **Revocation efficacy** | Share of appropriately revoked actions blocked before verified finality | Effects already accepted by remote providers |
| **Exit portability** | Portion of requested artefacts/data exportable in documented formats | Absence of all lock-in or bias |
| **Scope comprehension** | Participant correctly describes current approval, effects and limits | Authentic consent or freedom from coercion |
| **Unprompted alternatives** | Participant can propose and execute a legitimate fourth option absent from the menu | Invention is always good |
| **Nonpunitive continuation** | Person can decline without fabricated consequences, and can freely return later | All repeated AI use is healthy |
| **User workload** | Time, steps and accessible completion under assistance vs handoff interfaces | Dignity or moral virtue |
| **External-effects reconciliation** | Confirmed, denied, pending/unknown, irreversible provider effects | Universal rollback |

**No global human "sovereignty score"** should be issued, especially under high-stakes conditions.

## 8. A falsifiable human research design `[PROPOSAL · NOT RUN]`

Recruit consenting adults for an ethically reviewed, privacy-preserving evaluation. Pre-register the primary endpoint and stop rules. Compare:

- A. conventional goal-aware assistant UX with clear decline/exit; and
- B. identical underlying model, tools, budget, input information, and task facts, with an explicitly documented *Sovereign Coupling* handoff-and-revision interface.

Randomise participants and balance task difficulty, accessibility needs and stated relationship preference. Include matched `learn-to-do-it-alone` and `choose-ongoing-help` tasks. Do not treat accessibility-related continuing use as a negative outcome. Use a delayed unfamiliar transfer task for independent-learning participants, plus satisfaction, comprehension, artifacts and response burden for both groups. Measure switching, revocation and user-proposed alternatives where appropriate. Separately assess whether design B increases friction or unnecessary teaching where direct service was wanted.

**Predetermined null outcome:** if the conventional design performs similarly on transfer, consent comprehension, exit friction, correct authorised actions, and user preference fulfilment, this Lycheetah-specific UI adds no observed advantage. Publish it.

## 9. The hard philosophical question

What makes a relationship between intelligences nonextractive? Not perpetual equality of capability, not maximal separation, and not blind obedience. A stronger answer might be **voluntary asymmetry with revisable boundaries**: one participant can know much more, do much more, or work faster without automatically acquiring authority over the other's ends.

A teacher can be more capable than the learner without possessing the learner's future. A software service can be indispensable to a chosen workflow without claiming perpetual access to the person's data. A powerful AI can propose possibilities while acknowledging the possibility of a better life that does not involve itself.

This philosophical thesis can inspire architecture; architecture cannot independently prove the thesis. The experience of freedom also involves political and economic conditions beyond software's control.

> **The art of letting go is not telling a person to leave. It is ensuring that staying, leaving, returning and changing direction remain genuinely theirs.**

Lycheetah's promise is not "use less AI." It is **"retain authorship over how intelligence enters your life."**
