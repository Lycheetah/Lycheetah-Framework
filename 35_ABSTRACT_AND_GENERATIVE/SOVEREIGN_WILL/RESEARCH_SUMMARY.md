# Sovereign Will — Full Eight-Forge Research Summary

## Central claim

**Intelligence should increase what humans can understand, create, choose and accomplish while preserving legitimate authority to disagree, revise, refuse, delegate, depart and return.**

The Framework does *not* claim that metaphysical free will has been mathematically solved. It distinguishes practical freedom, willpower, agency, and sovereignty, and builds restricted AI authority checks motivated by those concepts.

## Discoveries by phase

- **Forge I — Foundational charter.** Practical free will as meaningful deliberation; willpower as persistence *or redirection*; agency as consequential action; sovereignty as retained authority. No human dignity metric.
- **Forge II — Revocable Sovereign Choice Protocol.** Synthetic one-use, versioned, scoped permissions and precise action approval. A previous instruction cannot automatically outrank a revised one.
- **Forge III — Commit-time counterfactuals.** A weak pre-dispatch check can allow an effect after revocation; a protected local finality fence blocks it when revocation happens first. Does not cancel already-committed remote actions.
- **Forge IV — Intent-to-authority bridge.** Research wrapper mapped revised intentions to an existing Lycheetah CommitFence interface. Initial tests were contract simulations; upstream tests were prepared but not yet run.
- **Forge V — Real upstream integration and atomic journal.** Actual GitHub `CommitFence` and `research_support.Action` sources used for local integration. Intention-version and authority updates share one SQLite transaction, reducing crash-window inconsistency.
- **Forge VI — Signed authority and possibility.** Synthetic Ed25519 signed approvals for exact action/expiry plus concept of the **Counterfactual Agency Horizon**. Signing key identity is not proof of a person's informed, uncoerced consent.
- **Forge VII — Self-Displacement and Sovereign Coupling.** Independence cannot simply mean fewer AI interactions; chosen ongoing support and accessible exit both matter. In 18 scripted cases a strong conventional goal-aware baseline tied the branded policy 18/18; no demonstrated incremental advantage.
- **Forge VIII — Sovereignty Surface.** In eight paired fictional trajectories with identical usage history, seven yielded different requested-option reachability outcomes; one negative control stayed the same. No free-will score or human dependency diagnosis.

## Three dimensions of sovereignty

1. **Action:** Can a user authorise, scope, review and stop consequential actions?
2. **Epistemic:** Can a user scrutinise conclusions, discover alternatives and form an original dissent?
3. **Relational:** Can the user alter their relationship to AI, including departure, return and continued chosen assistance?

## MICROORCIM reformulation

The symbolic `microorcim-event` describes a meaningful moment of **affirmation, refusal, revision or origination**. The computed `microorcim-monitor` concerns specified observed drift or authority mismatch. Neither measures metaphysical freedom, consciousness or a person's worth. Historical formula variants are not mathematically interchangeable.

## Philosophical synthesis

- **Earned Light** — meaning through growth and honestly chosen participation, never coerced sacrifice.
- **Sovereign Will** — human freedom of direction and revision.
- **Intelligence Without Dominion** — capability does not justify retaining authority over the person it serves.
- **Sovereign Coupling** — freely chosen relationships and interdependence must remain open to modification, rather than mandating isolation.

## Why the work is valuable, and where it stops

The immediate practical value lies in a reproducible research programme for local agent authorization, revision, expiring approvals and option-reachability analysis. Similar permission and human-centred design concepts exist in established security engineering and autonomy research. This lineage is a Lycheetah-specific synthesis, not proof of first invention or superior real-world performance.

The decisive next experiment is an independent participant study comparing a sovereignty-focused interface with a strong conventional baseline on comprehension, safe refusal, meaningful revision, revocation, export, return rights and useful task completion. Security evaluation must separately check genuine authentication, privileged bypass, host trust boundaries and remote-provider commit semantics.
