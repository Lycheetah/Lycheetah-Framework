What you need to know

SXP-L transcripts begin with a people line declaring names. `X > Y :` means X speaks to Y; editor punctuation is not itself pronounced.

mi is I and ti is you, relative to the viewpoint in which they occur. liva means love; luma means miss; rava means reconnect with. devora closes a statement. gonape NAME dimotu explicitly names a person.

kelunaro introduces a request for clarification: the embedded statement is *proposed*, not asserted or verified. bakumo starts the embedded statement and tesavo ends that scope. gonape taba dimotu refers to the first utterance.

soraviko makes inner mi and ti refer to the speaker and addressee of the **earlier** utterance. tumerako makes them refer to the **current** questioner and addressee.

The fully named form expands the perspective markers into gonape NAME dimotu. JSON is a conventional explicit representation, while the English examples are controlled paraphrases. All reports are unverified; no sentence grants executable permission.

Choose “Unsure” rather than guessing when uncertain. There is no penalty for uncertainty in the raw export; the researcher chooses the scoring policy in advance.

Example using different practice names: Toma tells Rika “I love you.” Under soraviko ti rava mi, Rika is reconnecting with Toma. Under tumerako ti rava mi, Toma is reconnecting with Rika. Both are hypothetical clarification proposals.
