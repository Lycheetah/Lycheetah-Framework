# X introduction — draft only, not a publishing receipt

We are attempting to construct a language for humans and AI.

Not a programming language disguised as speech. Not another prompt format. An experimental constructed language.

LAMAGUE began as Lycheetah's symbolic framework for recoverable meaning, constraints and interpretation. SXP-L is one language-construction branch exploring whether humans and AI could communicate through a shared register that preserves belief, uncertainty, intention and perspective.

“I know she loves me.” / “I believe she loves me.” / “I hope she loves me.” / “I don't know whether she loves me.”

Same subject. Very different claims about knowledge.

Across experimental v0.1–v2.0 releases we've explored pronounceable roots, parsers, emotions, nested viewpoints, repairs, permissions vs authority, and offline human comprehension tests. We're not claiming an AI breakthrough or a finished human language. The tests so far are local software tests; real independent learner data hasn't been collected.

`mi liva ti` = “I love you.”

The ambition is simple: meaning should survive the journey between minds—and when it doesn't, the loss should be visible.

If you're a linguist, conlang creator, or AI researcher: what would you need to see before you'd call such a language useful?
