# Session reconstruction: SXP-L × LAMAGUE through v2.0

**Provenance:** narrative reconstruction from the visible working conversation, its summaries, and release records. This is **not** the verbatim transcript of the chat, and cannot substitute for a user-exported ChatGPT transcript. Direct participant quotations below are limited to short, specifically remembered statements.

## Founding intention
The user challenged the research team to attempt an entire synthetic language, not just a notation or a prompt convention. The design target was two-way human/AI communication: express real human life and emotions in a pronounceable register while retaining explicit machine-readable semantics and indicating uncertainty, loss, and scope. Project names are SXP-L × LAMAGUE, associated but not identical to Native-36/Core.

## Evolution (version checkpoints)
- **v0.1:** dual-register concept (human/spoken and canonical structured representation); early syntax, Native-36 influence, first specification.
- **v0.2:** executable Python parser, AST/canonical rendering, determinism and local round-trips.
- **v0.3:** nested content, affect, relations and explicit viewpoints; programmatic tests.
- **v0.4:** human-readable token expansions and early pronounceable form.
- **v0.5:** dialogue, stable semantic vocabulary, `liva` (love), `hira` (hope), `kena` (know), `loma` (longing), `luma` (miss); `mi liva ti` established as a key enduring expression.
- **v0.6:** compact `mi liva ti` register and scoped negation; **retained historical form** thereafter.
- **v0.7:** CV phonological proposals, spoken delimiters `devora`, `gonape ... dimotu`, `bakumo ... tesavo`; prototype speech approximations (not listening validation).
- **v0.8:** speech acts, conditionals, commitments, intention/desire, staying (`satoma`); legacy layering and compositional challenges.
- **v0.9:** speech reports around grants/denials/revocations; permission report `periva` is NOT itself authorization; separate capability gate.
- **v1.0:** sovereignty and roles for trust, advice, refusal, delegation, final human decision boundary; initial external v0.1–v1.0 Argon/Aetheron audit remains critical.
- **v1.1:** quotation and attribution without promoting quotes to truth or instruction; contrast and `borema` underdetermined causal relation.
- **v1.2:** cause/effect directional markers `larumeko` / `voraneku`; indirect speech.
- **v1.3:** long multi-turn speaker identities, names and replies (`norava`, `taviro`, `tevaro`, `rasufe`, `repira`); ambiguous reference abstention.
- **v1.4:** reported belief, reported speech, not-knowing whether (`naveriko`, `belareko`, `nesaluko`); scoped first-person resolution and independent restricted-reader tests.
- **v1.5:** Human Understanding Forge: participant-facing offline research instrument, SXP-L vs controlled English vs JSON, practice and scored questions; all data synthetic so far.
- **v1.6:** Generalization Forge: held-out combinations (`liva`, `luma`, `rava`), multi-turn transfer questions, 30 scored cases; not yet a real learner validation.
- **v1.7:** Living Conversation Forge: `.studio` authoring and local browser interface, repair annotation `?1` (ask clarification) and `~1` (self-correction), separate semantic sidecar for meaning unavailable in legacy `.sxh`.
- **v1.8:** Conversational Repair Forge: provisional pronounceable `kelunaro` and `remakivo`, explicit-name clarification proposals (so inner `mi` cannot accidentally shift to questioner), 49 local tests and 700 generated cases.
- **v1.9:** Natural Reference Forge: `soraviko` (prior speaker's viewpoint) and `tumerako` (current questioner's viewpoint), explicit recovery into v1.8 names, restricted subset, 71 tests, 400 generated cases, 80/80 synthetic independent-limited-reader agreement (naïve baseline 40/80).
- **v2.0:** Validation Forge: no new roots; 32 semantics-grounded scenarios and four representations—compact SXP-L, explicit-name SXP-L, controlled English, JSON—across four Latin-square cohorts. Offline participant HTML, separate researcher key/scorer, 12 new + 71 focused inherited tests, 4 synthetic Chromium runs. No genuine learners yet.

## Distinctive project decisions
1. `mi liva ti` = “I love you” stays *exactly* that; no replacement/aliasing to `mi jiva ti`.
2. Meaning preservation must be audited; when `.sxh` cannot represent a repair, emit a sidecar and loss ledger rather than falsely claiming perfect reversibility.
3. Language reports are not verification of statements, authorization, identity, or actual external outcomes.
4. Reject ambiguous references within bounded subsets; no silent guesses about pronouns.
5. Language quality is not software pass-count: demand unfamiliar human readers, listening tests and independently reviewed comparative baselines.
6. Make a language someone *wants* to speak, not merely an agent safety DSL; develop metaphor, stories, humour, mundane vocabulary and acoustic distinctiveness.
7. Preserve releases byte-for-byte, maintain compatibility documentation, and mark every result as proposed / local-tested / external-audited / human-validated / deployed.
