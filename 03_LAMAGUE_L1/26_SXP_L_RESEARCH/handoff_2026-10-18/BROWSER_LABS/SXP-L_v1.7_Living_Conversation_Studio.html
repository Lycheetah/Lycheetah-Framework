Read the notation

The first phrase

mi liva ti means “I love you.” In a dialogue, mi binds to the local viewpoint holder and ti to that viewpoint’s addressee. We have not changed this expression.

Named people and third-person ambiguity

sivena means a gender-neutral third person. With two possible people it remains ambiguous. Adding #sela explicitly anchors it to Sela. The preview never guesses from recency.

Nested reported perspectives

^belareko(maka,nara) introduces a reported belief held by Maka, locally addressed to Nara. In the following leaf, mi refers to Maka even if Sela is the outer narrator. Reported belief is not established fact.

Important meaning-loss boundary

?1 and ~1 belong to the experimental Studio authoring layer, not the inherited spoken SXP-L grammar. Exporting only an old .sxh transcript collapses both to an ordinary reply link. The Python compiler writes a SHA-256-bound .sxpl17.json companion that preserves their distinction. A hash detects changes but does not authenticate a human speaker.

Limits and non-claims

This browser preview supports present declarative leaves of the form mi liva/luma/rava ti/sivena, optionally with one explicit anchor and perspective frames. It does not understand arbitrary English, validate actual audio, grant permission, or evaluate whether beliefs are true. This is an educational local preview, not an independent human-comprehension study.
