The Generalization Forge

Can learners transfer familiar SXP-L rules to new verbs, names, and three-turn conversation contexts?

Independent comprehension pilot

This is an experimental offline prototype, not a validated psychometric test. You will see a short tutorial, ten unscored practice items, and 30 scored questions. Allow around 20–40 minutes; feel free to withdraw.

For adult volunteers (18+). Participation is optional. Do not enter a real name, contact details, or sensitive information. Nothing is transmitted. Answers stay in this browser tab until you explicitly export a CSV. Closing the tab loses the data. Withdraw any time without exporting.

For comparisons, the facilitator should allocate successive volunteers A, B, C in rotation. Each arm has 10 trials per register and two of each task type per register. Participants should not choose based on preference.

The three equivalent reading formats

All trials describe a three-turn synthetic conversation. The final turn may explicitly reply to Turn 1 or Turn 2, or may not point to a prior turn at all. A reply link is not necessarily agreement. Each final clause uses liva (love), luma (miss), or rava (reconnect with). A reported belief or uncertainty is not automatically a fact, and no transcript grants permission to act.

SXP-L vocabulary
norava declares a person
gonape X dimotu names X
taviro begins a turn
tevaro ends a turn
devora ends a sentence
Perspective operators
naveriko reportedly conveyed
belareko attributed belief
nesaluko not knowing whether
Each operator names a holder and local addressee.
Reference and care
mi local first person (I)
ti local second person (you)
liva love
luma miss someone
rava reconnect with
sivena a third person
rasufe explicit third-person anchor
repira gonape taba dimotu explicit reply to Turn 1; tabe names Turn 2

Scope rule: the last perspective operator names who “mi” and “ti” refer to in the final clause. If no operator appears, use the outer speaker and addressee. For sivena, exclude the local speaker and addressee. With one remaining named candidate it is uniquely inferred; with multiple candidates it remains ambiguous unless rasufe names the person.

Always answer about the final turn, not the earlier two. The lesson example uses liva; the scored sentences combine the same grammar with luma and rava. In controlled English, read the perspective lines in order. In JSON, read the perspective_frames array in order and use its last element. English, JSON and SXP-L show the same invented scenario.

Example: a nested belief (all three formats)

Answer: Sela is local “I”, and the innermost perspective is belief.

norava gonape maka dimotu
norava gonape nara dimotu
norava gonape sela dimotu
norava gonape vera dimotu
taviro gonape maka dimotu gonape nara dimotu
naveriko gonape nara dimotu gonape maka dimotu
belareko gonape sela dimotu gonape vera dimotu
mi liva ti devora
tevaro

Controlled English: Maka to Nara. Nara reportedly conveyed something to Maka. Within it, Sela is attributed a belief, addressed locally to Vera. Final clause: “I love you.”
