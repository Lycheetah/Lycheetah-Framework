## Mission

Anon Politician is intended as an AI-assisted democratic policy intelligence system for Aotearoa New Zealand. It should help citizens and elected representatives connect recurring public input, evidence, CASCADE-style knowledge/dependency tracking, adversarial critique from competing policy perspectives, future-scenario analysis, transparent policy revision, and later outcome tracking. AI assists; accountable humans make public decisions.

The founder's incentive hypothesis is that listening to the public, explaining trade-offs, improving proposals and delivering outcomes might become a competitive advantage in leadership. Treat that as a testable hypothesis, not a guarantee.

## Latest new concept: three civic signal lanes

The recurring citizen-input mechanism should distinguish:

- **Open public voice:** opt-in input for issue discovery and participant views, not automatically a population estimate.
- **Population measurement:** a properly designed survey for a specified population, with documented selection, response/nonresponse, analysis and uncertainty.
- **Informed deliberation:** a transparent, inclusive citizen panel with balanced evidence, questioning and discussion; its recommendations are not automatically a population poll.

Stakeholder submissions and formal statutory consultations remain distinct. The new validator flags unsupported representative/consensus claims based on declared metadata; it cannot certify a real sample. A public decision-response ledger traces what decision-makers heard, changed or declined, why, and what happens next.

The Ministry's official page records 56 submissions to the 2025–26 consultation and summarises council support and mixed developer views; these submissions are not a representative survey of New Zealanders. The Ministry says the Bill is being prepared for introduction in the first quarter of 2027; recheck current materials when milestones change. Sources are in each case/kit source register.

The Treasury's Living Standards Framework and CBAx are established baselines. Do not claim Anon Politician invented wellbeing analysis or cost-benefit analysis. The contribution to test is the connected continuity loop: recurring public signals, symmetric challenge, claim/dependency history, visible disposition of input, and later comparison with outcomes.

## Development status and honest limits

- CASCADE's prototype preserves dependencies and queues changed upstream assumptions for review rather than automatically deleting or falsifying claims.
- v0.2 defines a controlled three-arm comparison (ordinary analysis, structured evidence ledger, full workflow), but no clean independent evaluation is yet complete.
- Civic Signal v0.3 has 16 passing unit tests for declared-metadata checks; it does not certify representativeness or show real policy impact.
- HTML JavaScript syntax and static control references check out; a true interactive browser test remains outstanding.
- No real public participant data is collected. Do not collect it before legal, privacy, security, accessibility and governance reviews.
- The original transcript's Pyramid Coherence Score of 0.98 and “validated” claims are not established by reproducible independent evidence in the materials reviewed.

## Non-negotiable discipline

- Separate source fact, interpretation, hypothesis, value judgement, preference and unknown.
- No political party owns the truth; don't rank or endorse political actors.
- Test the founder's assumptions as rigorously as government/party claims.
- Do not treat voluntary polls as national consensus or forecasts as measured outcomes.
- Preserve minority concerns and unresolved contradictions; do not let automated summaries erase them.
- AI is not the accountable decision-maker.
- Compare complex workflows against simpler methods, and simplify if no defensible advantage appears.
