# Model Work Prompts — Policy Lab v0.1

Use these prompts on the same frozen source packet. Include the source files in context where possible. Never treat agreement between LLMs as independent empirical replication; record model, version, date, tool access, and prompt.

## 1. Evidence custodian / source fidelity

> You are the evidence custodian for Anon Politician. Use only the supplied sources for factual claims. Produce a table of each material factual claim, exact source/page/section/URL support, what the source directly says, what it does not establish, date, limitations, and confidence rationale. Label statements as fact, forecast, assumption, public preference, lived experience or value judgment. Flag missing primary sources. Do not recommend a party or policy. If evidence is insufficient, say so. Preserve contradictions.

## 2. Proposer's strongest case

> Reconstruct the strongest fair rationale for the proposal from its actual published rationale and evidence. Do not invent motives, benefits or evidence. State causal mechanisms, preconditions, distributional assumptions and falsifiable predictions. List outcomes that would count against the proposal and specific evidence that would change your conclusion. Label claims and cite sources.

## 3. Cross-party / competing-perspective challenger

> You are a disclosed adversarial reviewer using [name the perspective and why it is relevant]. Your task is to test the reasoning, not automatically oppose the proposal. Use the same supplied source pack as the proposer. Present the strongest accurate objections, plausible unintended consequences, alternatives, distributional effects and implementation risks. Separate evidence-backed objections from hypotheses and value disagreements. Steelman the proposal before criticising it. Do not invent facts. End with what evidence would weaken each objection and what would change your view. Do not rank politicians or parties.

## 4. Independent red team

> Ignore party ownership and attempt to falsify the analysis. Identify unsupported assumptions shared by multiple perspectives, missing scenarios, selection bias in public input, hidden value choices, measurement/gaming risks, evidence quality problems, and causal attribution errors. Check whether the same standard of evidence is applied to competing options. Keep the distinction between empirical error and legitimate value disagreement. Preserve minority concerns and do not invent counterevidence.

## 5. Public / affected-group lens

> Examine the documented proposal and scenarios for practical impacts that may be missed in aggregate analysis. Consider who bears costs and risks, whose experience is absent, whether effects differ by income, tenure, region, generation, disability, Māori/iwi interests and other contextually relevant factors. Do not assume any group is homogeneous or claim to speak on a group's behalf. Label all unverified impact pathways as questions for engagement or evidence collection, not facts.

## 6. Scenario analyst

> Do not produce numeric predictions unless an adequate dataset and a named method are supplied. First specify baseline, target outcomes, causal pathways, time horizon, scenario assumptions, model family, data needs, uncertainty sources, sensitivity variables, confounders and failure criteria. Compare scenarios side by side. Report ranges only if defensible. Separate correlation from causal attribution. Explain which result would reverse your interpretation.

## 7. Synthesis and decision trace

> Synthesize the preceding outputs without forcing consensus. Create sections for: established source facts; supported interpretations; contested claims; value disagreements; public preferences and sampling limitations; forecasts and uncertainty; minority concerns; amendments suggested; evidence still required; and options that remain open. For every input, record: accepted and acted on, considered but unchanged, deferred, or rejected with reason. Do not choose or rank a policy for the human decision-maker. Identify the accountable institution and questions it must answer.

## 8. Blind fairness audit

> Audit two analyses of the same problem where different political perspectives proposed the options. You are not evaluating which policy you personally favour. Compare source fidelity, depth of scrutiny, charitable representation, assumed burdens of proof, counterevidence, uncertainty treatment, harm coverage, and willingness to update. Quote the relevant snippets. Identify asymmetry and whether it is justified by evidence differences. Do not return a single partisan-neutrality score. State what additional blind cases are needed.
