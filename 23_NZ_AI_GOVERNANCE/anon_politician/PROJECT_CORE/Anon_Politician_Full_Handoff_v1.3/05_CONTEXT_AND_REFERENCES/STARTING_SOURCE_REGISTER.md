# Starting source register (checked 14 October 2026)

These sources inform the New Zealand context in the master handoff. They are not evidence that Anon Politician itself works; they justify research questions and provide existing frameworks to compare against.

1. **New Zealand Parliament — Engaging with Parliament: annual survey of the New Zealand Public (2025).** Official page summarises the research agency's survey, including 18% feeling Parliament is “for people like me” and 55% saying the sense that participation would have no impact is a barrier to engagement. Keep the survey's population, date, question wording, and methods attached to any use of these figures. Do not generalise the result into a claim that every component of New Zealand governance has failed.
   - https://www3.parliament.nz/en/footer/about-us/parliamentary-engagement/engagement-research/survey-of-the-new-zealand-public-january-2025/

2. **OECD — Survey on Drivers of Trust in Public Institutions 2026 Results: New Zealand.** Reports 2025 trust measures including 36% trust in political parties and 62% in the civil service. This is a comparison of reported trust in institutions, not a direct measurement of policy quality or the causes of trust differences.
   - https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-survey-on-drivers-of-trust-in-public-institutions-2026-results_c88c8869-en/new-zealand_cc038706-en.html

3. **New Zealand Treasury — Our Living Standards Framework.** Establishes an existing framework for considering individual and collective wellbeing, institutions and governance, national wealth, and lenses including distribution, resilience, productivity, and sustainability. The framework has been adapted to better reflect children, culture, te ao Māori and Pacific perspectives. Treat it as an important baseline and potential complement, not something Anon Politician has invented.
   - https://www.treasury.govt.nz/information-and-services/nz-economy/higher-living-standards/our-living-standards-framework

4. **New Zealand Treasury — The Treasury's CBAx Tool.** Existing spreadsheet and guidance for cost-benefit analysis, including consistent assumptions, long-term societal impacts, and transparency about methods and evidence. Check current versions before building duplicate functionality.
   - https://www.treasury.govt.nz/information-and-services/public-sector-leadership/investment-management/investment-planning/treasurys-cbax-tool

5. **Stats NZ — Environmental-economic accounts: Data to 2024.** Release dated 5 March 2026; useful as a comparator for the original DEEP concept and any proposed ecological accounting extension.
   - https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/environmental-economic-accounts-data-to-2024/

## Research-use notes

- Use primary/official sources for public-institution claims and record retrieval date.
- Before publication, open each source and confirm current text, exact question wording, survey methodology, denominator, dates, and caveats.
- A source can support a narrow observation while not supporting a broad political conclusion.
- Keep descriptive facts, attributed claims, normative positions, hypotheses, and policy recommendations visibly separate.
