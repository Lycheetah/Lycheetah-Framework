# Anon Politician — executive brief

Anon Politician is an early-stage proposal for an AI-assisted democratic policy intelligence system in Aotearoa New Zealand. It is not intended to make policy decisions. It is meant to help people and elected representatives make better-informed decisions through structured public input, evidence, competing perspectives, forecasts, and transparent accountability.

The founder's key insight is that political disagreement need not mean an idea is discarded because of its source. A policy should be able to face serious opposing critique, respond to evidence, be revised, and be assessed after implementation. The founder proposes regular—potentially monthly—citizen polls or consultation questions chosen by politicians, with citizen input feeding an analytical process and a clear record of what decision-makers did with the input.

The intended workflow connects public opinion, sourced claims, CASCADE dependency-aware knowledge, AURA-inspired explicit values, adversarial AI representing different policy perspectives, scenario analysis, human decisions, and outcome tracking. A party could help provide a challenger model, but neither party should control the system; independent scrutiny and political-symmetry testing are required.

The desired incentive is to make transparent, responsive, evidence-informed leadership competitive and legible to the public. This remains a hypothesis to test. Public opinion is not identical to national interest, empirical truth, legal rights, or representative consensus; poll methods, underrepresented groups, minority views, uncertainty, privacy, and long-term impacts must be handled explicitly.

The original framework uses AURA Protocol and Pyramid Cascade Architecture/CASCADE. AURA names Trust Entropy, Value-Transfer Ratio, Purpose Alignment Index and a constructive-alternative concept called Vector Inversion. CASCADE structures Foundation, Theory and Edge claims and traces dependencies. Those concepts are not yet independently validated policy instruments. The prototype's 11 unit tests and 12 synthetic benchmark cases only establish limited code-conformance behaviour.

**Next step:** select one tractable New Zealand policy demonstrator, create a traceable evidence and public-input record, apply at least two adversarial perspectives, compare scenarios, document revisions, and evaluate the process against ordinary policy analysis and a static evidence ledger. AI supports; elected humans remain accountable.
