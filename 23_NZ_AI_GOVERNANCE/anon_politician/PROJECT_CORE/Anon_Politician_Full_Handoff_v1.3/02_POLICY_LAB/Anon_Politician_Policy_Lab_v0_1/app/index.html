Housing growth and development levies

Pilot case · Oct 2026

Can New Zealand design the funding of housing-growth infrastructure so the costs, incentives and benefits are transparent, while helping more homes get built and treating affected groups fairly?

3 pillars

The government's Going for Housing Growth programme covers land/planning, infrastructure funding and incentives for councils/communities. Official source

Q1 2027

The Ministry says the development-levies bill is being prepared for introduction in the first quarter of 2027. Official source

Input ahead

Further input is expected on calculation methods and disclosure, plus a select-committee process after introduction. Timing may change. Official source

Known government position, stated neutrally

The programme proposes replacing development contributions with development levies intended to recover a proportionate share of growth-related capital costs. The official description says levies would be calculated by levy area, separated by service, subject to oversight, and potentially phased in by councils.

This describes the published proposal; it does not establish whether the design will achieve its stated objectives.

Public participation design

Capture a public signal — not a fake consensus

No responses loaded

Use this space to document an actual survey or consultation once one exists. All fields below start empty because this prototype has collected no citizen responses.

Core rule

A monthly public pulse is an input signal, not automatically the public's consensus. Report who participated, who did not, how questions were worded, how views changed after participants saw balanced information, and what remains disputed.

Constructive disagreement

Challenge the proposal, not the people

Multi-perspective

Model each perspective as an auditable role. A party-supplied or party-configured challenger can contribute, but must not be the sole red team or final arbiter. All roles must use the same evidence packet and must be checked for factual accuracy and unequal scrutiny.

A · Proposer's strongest case

Describe objectives and causal mechanism; cite evidence; identify who should benefit; state what would falsify the proposal's rationale.

B · Cross-party challenge

Use the strongest plausible opposing policy perspective; identify trade-offs and counterevidence without inventing motives or facts.

C · Independent red team

Look for missing alternatives, implementation failure, perverse incentives, distributional effects and assumptions shared by both parties.

D · Public / lived-experience lens

Identify practical impacts, barriers, unexpected harms and whose experience is missing; do not treat any group as internally uniform.

Fairness check: give each model the same core evidence; rotate which perspective authors the proposal; evaluate with blinded cases from multiple policy domains. Party-origin is not evidence of truth or falsehood.
Future trajectories

Compare scenarios without pretending to predict certainty

No forecasts supplied

Populate only after specifying a model, data, baseline and horizon. The rows below are scenario families for research, not measured effects or formal government alternatives.

Scenario 0 · Status quo / baseline

Estimate likely trajectory if proposed changes do not proceed, using a defensible baseline and a clearly stated horizon.

Scenario 1 · Published levy design

Evaluate the proposed development-levy mechanism using the official formula once available and appropriate data.

Scenario 2 · Alternative allocation

Test a clearly specified alternative allocation of costs. State whether it is an analytical counterfactual, a stakeholder proposal or an official option.

Scenario 3 · Sensitivity / stress test

Vary build costs, interest rates, demand, infrastructure timing, uptake and other plausible drivers.

Prediction is not proof

Show ranges and alternative explanations. After implementation, compare forecasts with measured outcomes, investigate misses, and update dependent claims through CASCADE. Never convert model confidence into a political recommendation by default.

What this tool intentionally does not do

Current limits

  • It does not collect or publish real citizen votes.
  • It does not call an LLM or forecast a policy outcome.
  • It does not infer a consensus from a voluntary poll.
  • It does not rank politicians, parties or policies.
  • It does not replace legal consultation, expert analysis, elected decision-making or public scrutiny.

Those would be separate, governed integrations requiring privacy, security, evaluation and legal review.
