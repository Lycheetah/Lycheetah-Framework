# Development Levies — Public Question Design (Draft v0.1)

**Status:** Draft for research and community review; not a live consultation and not legal advice.  
**Policy question:** What information and design safeguards would help people judge how growth-related infrastructure costs should be allocated and disclosed?

## Source context to publish before asking

The Ministry for Cities, Environment, Regions and Transport describes a proposed system of charges to help pay for infrastructure associated with growth. Services listed include water supply, wastewater, stormwater, transport, reserves and community infrastructure. The policy is intended to improve the way infrastructure is funded to support housing/urban growth. The Ministry's summary of 56 submissions describes strong support for replacement among local-government submitters and mixed developer views. Those submissions are not a representative national poll. The Ministry also describes the role of the Commerce Commission in developing levy calculation methodology and oversight.

**Make the distinction clear:** the government's stated objective is not proof that housing affordability or supply will improve. The supplied sources do not establish the final effects on house prices, rents, project feasibility or the share of costs ultimately borne by each group.

## Questions (use in this order where the chosen channel permits)

### 1. Open priority

**What is most important for decision-makers to get right when deciding how growth-related infrastructure is funded?**

- Keeping homes and new development financially feasible
- Ensuring the infrastructure needed for growth is funded and delivered on time
- Preventing existing ratepayers from carrying costs caused by growth
- Ensuring charges are predictable and understandable before land is bought or projects begin
- Making sure charges reflect local infrastructure costs rather than unfairly shifting costs between areas
- Protecting access to housing and infrastructure for groups or communities that may otherwise be disadvantaged
- Transparency, independent scrutiny and the ability to challenge calculations
- Something else (please describe)
- Not sure / need more information

Do not force respondents to rank options if the intention is only to discover themes. If ranking is used, pre-test the order and clarify that the options may conflict.

### 2. Why it matters

**What experience, concern or evidence led you to choose that priority?**

Optional response. Tell people not to include sensitive personal information. State how open-text responses will be handled.

### 3. Source comprehension

After a plain-language, balanced explanation, ask: **Which parts of the proposed system do you still need explained before judging it?**

- How the amount of a levy would be calculated
- Which area or development would be charged
- How future growth and long-lived infrastructure projects would be treated
- How councils would show how money was spent
- How levies could affect new housing projects and affordability
- How existing ratepayers are protected from funding growth-related costs
- How Māori land and Māori housing would be affected
- How the regulator can challenge an incorrect or unfair calculation
- Something else / still not sure

### 4. Trade-off understanding

**Which risk would you most want the rules to guard against?**

- Charging a project for infrastructure that is not needed or does not proceed
- Leaving growth-related infrastructure costs with existing ratepayers
- Setting charges so unpredictably that projects become harder to plan
- Applying broad area charges that shift costs between locations unfairly
- Applying overly narrow rules that prevent councils from responding to changing growth
- Delays or complexity that prevent infrastructure or housing from being delivered
- Another risk / not sure

This is a risk-priority question, not a claim about which risk is most probable.

### 5. Informed preference

**Having read the information above, what further safeguard would you most like decision-makers to consider?**

Allow free text and an explicit "no additional safeguard / not sure" option. Candidate safeguards could include published calculation workbooks, independent review, plain-language disclosure, transparent appeals, periodic recalibration, transition protections, or reporting actual outcomes against forecasts. Do not frame these as all already available or formally proposed unless the relevant source confirms that.

Where the method permits, ask the initial priority again after the information page using the same wording. Report changes and non-changes; neither response is automatically more correct.

### 6. Follow-up

**What evidence would most change your view?**

For example, a worked levy calculation, an independent assessment of likely cost incidence, evidence about project feasibility, a council infrastructure plan, or later outcomes data.

## Method warning

These questions are a design draft. An open online version describes participating respondents only. It cannot be labelled a representative view of New Zealand without a separate appropriate sample and analysis design. A deliberative panel should have balanced material, expert questioning and time to discuss trade-offs. Formal submissions must continue through the official statutory process where applicable.
