## 1. The reader's promise

The reader enters contemporary, rain-dark Dunedin through the life of an ordinary
archive worker and discovers that records, names and routes are physical forces in an
ancient conflict over a simple question:

> Does understanding a living thing create relationship—or the right to control it?

The world must be understandable before it is explained. A reader can follow a cat,
fear a closing door, recognize a political threat, love a companion and solve an
archival clue without knowing the formal architecture beneath those events.

## 3. What reality is

### The Field

The Field is not a god, ruler, simulation or conscious judge. It is the relational
substrate through which things can meet, affect one another and remain themselves.

In ordinary life it is invisible. Under pressure it becomes perceptible through:

- resonance in architecture or objects;
- routes that appear between deeply related places;
- names felt as coherent structures rather than definitions;
- contradictions that physically destabilize a false arrangement;
- echoes left by sustained attention, harm, repair or promise.

The Field does not reward virtue or punish sin. Cruel structures can survive for
centuries when materially supported. "False things collapse" is therefore not instant
cosmic justice; it means contradictions accumulate costs that someone eventually has
to carry.

### The Veil

The Veil is the perceptual consequence of the First Fracture. It is not one curtain
covering one secret world. It is the distance between a being's reality and an
observer's earned capacity to read it.

Different people can stand beside the same creature and perceive different degrees of
form. The unread is not invisible. It appears as silhouette, pressure, missing detail,
wrong scale, displaced weather or a shape the eye repeatedly fails to retain.

The Veil protects mystery from seizure, but it also causes danger. A person can harm a
being they cannot read; a creature can cross a human settlement whose warnings nobody
understands. This is why honest schools and coercive catalogues both arose.

### Recognition

Recognition occurs when attention, evidence and relationship reach enough coherence
for the observer to perceive a being more truthfully.

Recognition cannot be transferred as a mere label. A teacher can show evidence and
correct method; the learner must still encounter the being. Once earned, recognition
can deepen, fail under new conditions or be corrected. It is not a permanent rank.

Misrecognition is possible and dangerous. The School records corrected recognitions
publicly because sincere attention does not guarantee a correct conclusion.

## 4. Names

Four different things are commonly confused:

1. **Given name:** what a person or community asks to be called in ordinary life.
2. **Kind-name:** a useful shared description such as river-being or Waybearer.
3. **Received name:** a relational understanding freely revealed through sustained
   encounter.
4. **Final name:** the Concordance's claim that a being can be fully closed inside one
   authoritative definition.

A received name creates a route of mutual intelligibility. It does not grant command.
The relationship can be refused, withdrawn or changed.

Stealing a name means extracting the structure of recognition while removing the
being's freedom to decide how that understanding may be used. This can enable tracking,
binding, forced form, route denial and replication of a being's capacities.

The Lycheetah's full received name remains outside Volume I's printed language.

## 5. The Long Light

The Long Light is the surviving route created when grief entered the first broken fire.
It is neither a beam nor a universal rescue. It appears as pale gold relation between
places, people, promises or discoveries that remain capable of honest return.

Properties:

- it cannot force a crossing;
- it grows clearer through truthful attention and dimmer through extraction;
- it can outlast the people who first made the relation;
- it preserves a possible way through fracture without pretending the fracture never
  happened;
- it can be counterfeited visually, but false Long Light does not sustain a return
  journey.

Doors formed where the Lycheetah carried the Long Light through the world. The Mystery
School grew around a network of these doors.

## 6. The Nine living fragments

The Nine are not collectible batteries or a checklist of superpowers. Each is a broken
capacity once held in relation with the others. Civilisations developed around partial
fragments and mistook a fragment for the whole.

| Fragment | Plain story function | Distortion when isolated |
|---|---|---|
| Witness | knowing through evidence and attention | surveillance; knowledge without relationship |
| Measure | law, comparison and repeatable protection | scoring life until exceptions become crimes |
| Tongue | translation and shared language | the belief that naming exhausts meaning |
| Return | correction, revision and causal repair | endless correction with no forgiveness or rest |
| Open Hand | agency, consent and chosen relation | refusal of all obligation or shared consequence |
| Ember | the cost and endurance of illumination | glorifying suffering as proof of worth |
| Unfound | discovery, emergence and the not-yet-readable | novelty worship; mystery used to evade evidence |
| Furnace | transformation without loss of living essence | forced purification; destruction called growth |
| True Note | contradiction made audible; false structures ring against it | purity law; one tone used to silence every harmony |

These English labels are authoring handles, not necessarily the ancient names printed
in dialogue. Volume I should reveal their effects before presenting a formal list.

The creation account says the Lycheetah carried the Nine into keeping. Later evidence
may show that it carried routes between them rather than owning the fragments
themselves.

## 7. Death, memory and the forbidden shortcut

Death is real. The world has no reliable resurrection system.

A life can leave Field Echoes: pressure, sensory residue, unfinished routes, habits in
rooms, or words that become newly audible under matching conditions. An echo is not
automatically the continuing consciousness of the dead. Grief, desire and deliberate
forgery can all cause people to mistake an echo for a person.

The ancient King beneath the Archive seeks to defeat loss by assembling enough names,
echoes and routes to reconstruct the dead without remainder. His tragedy is not that he
grieves. It is that he eventually treats every living being as material for closing the
one absence he cannot accept.

No character returns cheaply from death. Apparent return must ask whether continuity,
memory, relation and choice survived—or whether grief built an eloquent copy.

## 8. The creation account and its uncertainty

`Before the First Name` is the clearest surviving account of First Recognition and the
First Fracture. Within the fiction, it is scripture translated through the School's
oldest witness tradition. It gives the reader a valid surface history:

- two first humans;
- the Lycheetah's freely revealed name;
- fear of loss;
- theft;
- the Ninefold fracture;
- the Veil;
- the Long Light and the first doors.

It is not guaranteed to be an omniscient transcript. Later volumes may challenge:

- whether there were only two humans;
- whether the thief acted alone;
- whether the Nine split in the theft or were already distinct;
- whether the Lycheetah chose to carry them or was accused of taking them;
- whose descendants preserved the written account;
- why the second human disappears from official history.

The emotional and moral event remains true even if its recorded arrangement changes.

## 9. Eras

### I. The Age Before Names

The Field is encountered directly. Creatures and first peoples learn one another
without inherited language. Geography is relational and not yet fixed into current
maps.

### II. First Recognition

The Lycheetah reveals a true name. Colour and intelligibility enter the world through
earned attention. The First Covenant is established.

### III. The First Fracture

A received name is seized in fear of death. The Nine separate, the Veil falls and
languages divide around partial truths.

### IV. The Long Dark

Peoples migrate with incomplete forms of knowledge. Creatures become unreadable.
Local recognitions, cults, craft traditions, false teachers and protective customs
develop. The Lycheetah runs the Ninefold routes and leaves doors.

### V. The Houses of Measure

Repeated disasters produce institutions devoted to evidence, stable routes and
corrected knowledge. Their work saves lives. The Index Bell is built to hold evacuation
routes during catastrophic route storms.

### VI. The Schism

One faction insists measures must remain answerable to living exceptions and becomes
the Mystery School. Another insists life must be made answerable to stable measures and
becomes the Pale Concordance. Both retain incomplete records and blame the other for
the deaths surrounding the split.

### VII. The Quiet Catalogue

The Concordance abandons open conquest. It enters registries, shipping systems,
universities, insurance tables, museum classifications and municipal surveys. By
controlling records, it slowly controls which routes and beings count as lawful.

### VIII. The Present Opening

Mara's scan activates or completes a transfer hidden inside the South Archive. The
Index Bell rings. Seven people vanish. A missing fragment and an open route reveal that
the Concordance's cataloguing war has already reached beyond Dunedin.

## 10. Geography and spatial law

### One world with folded relations

The default model is one material reality containing places joined by non-Euclidean
routes—not a stack of disposable parallel universes. A door can connect Dunedin to a
red desert because historical, linguistic or creature routes once made those places
adjacent in the Field.

Crossing does not teleport a person outside causality. Travel has duration, risk,
weather, checkpoints, bodily cost and the possibility of losing the return route.

### Dunedin

Literal Dunedin is the first earthly threshold: rain, volcanic stone, harbour weather,
steep streets, archives and a city whose mapped Octagon conceals an impossible seventh
corner. The city must be rendered with geographic care, not used as generic gothic
scenery.

### The South Archive

A public-facing institutional archive holding colonial trade, shipping and civic
records. Its mundane bureaucracy is exactly why the Concordance used it: records can
carry power while appearing too boring to fear.

The building has its own staff culture, funding pressure, provenance disputes and
ethical debts. Mara's work there must remain credible archival labour.

### The Mystery School

The School occupies a route nexus rather than a conventional underground footprint.
Its great hall connects lived teaching spaces, public exits, dormitories, kitchens,
workshops, infirmary rooms, creature courtyards, observatories and archives.

The Foundation Archive winds beneath its public memory. It preserves contradictions,
failed classifications and objects whose meaning remains unresolved.

### The City of Broken Names

The black-glass road leads toward a city governed by nine towers that stabilize one
approved reality. Statues have had their faces removed because public identity belongs
to function, not person. The city is not uniformly miserable: it is orderly, safe in
specific ways, technologically capable and home to people who believe the Concordance
ended generations of predation and route disaster.

Its provisional common name is **Veyl Tareth**, but the printed Volume I name remains
open for Mac's judgment.

### Later collection regions

The collection-derived domains remain future geographic/historical candidates:

- Tears: coast or drowned quarter where grief is witnessed without harvest;
- Shadows: red-black fracture territory shaped by rebellion;
- Silent Sentinel: grave thresholds and solitary watch;
- Veritas Contrast: courts of black/alabaster separation;
- Spellbound: celestial grammar, sigil craft and night architecture;
- Liminal Royalty: a border realm where titles cannot cross unchanged;
- Chaos Lit: storm country where plurality must find coherence;
- Pride: the fire-crown tradition of sovereignty without rule;
- Primal Echo / Future Light: frontier where ancient form and emergent form meet.

They must become inhabited places with economies and peoples, not themed levels.

## 11. Doors and routes

Every durable route has four elements:

1. **Origin:** a place or relation from which departure is possible.
2. **Bearing:** why these places can touch.
3. **Witness:** a living being, object or record that can verify the relation.
4. **Return:** the conditions under which the crossing can be reversed.

Concordance routes prioritize fixed coordinates and authorized custodians. School
routes prioritize living witnesses and visible exits. Both can fail.

Waybearers are beings whose bodies can hold several bearings without collapsing them
into one coordinate. This makes them essential to free travel and valuable targets for
the Final Catalogue.

## 12. Power and its limits

There is no generic spellcasting reservoir. Effects arise through specific relations:

- attention can reveal or wound;
- names can open relation or enforce chains;
- records can establish lawful routes when institutions and witnesses sustain them;
- contradiction can destabilize rigid mechanisms;
- form can shift when context, relationship or danger calls a truthful manifestation;
- artifacts compress a practiced operation but retain a cost and failure mode;
- creatures possess capacities humans can learn from but not automatically command.

Limits:

- knowledge without contact produces brittle approximations;
- a person cannot recognize every being equally;
- form changes cost the Lycheetah coherence, rest or access to other forms;
- routes can strand travellers;
- echoes can be forged or misread;
- contradiction alone does not defeat power unless someone can act through the opening;
- the School can be wrong;
- the Concordance's measurements often work;
- no prophecy removes the need to choose.

## 13. Bind, Strip and Salvage

These choices occur only after a confrontation creates a real boundary decision.

### Bind

Enter a voluntary relationship with continuing mutual consequence. A proper Bind is
loose enough to leave and strong enough to carry responsibility. It does not create
ownership or guarantee affection.

### Strip

Take a capacity, pattern or weapon from the defeated being into oneself. Strip may be
necessary when a power cannot safely remain where it is, but the taker inherits cost,
memory pressure and the risk of becoming organized around what was taken.

### Salvage

Return broken matter, tools or structures to making. Salvage can be merciful, practical
or evasive. It may preserve a future while refusing relationship with what was lost.

None is the righteous button. Motive, consent, necessity and later consequence decide
what the choice becomes.

## 14. Creature ecology

"Creature" does not mean lesser life. It is the broad human term for beings whose
forms, languages or field relations differ from ordinary human expectation.

Each being needs:

- habitat and material needs;
- signs or tracks that can be learned;
- boundaries and warning behavior;
- social relations within or beyond its kind;
- a reason humans misread it;
- an existence independent of what it can give a protagonist.

Unread beings appear as truthful silhouettes rather than generic monsters. Recognition
reveals difference; it does not make the being more human.

## 15. Institutions

### The Mystery School

Purpose: teach perception, correction and responsible choice without claiming final
authority.

Strengths:

- exits displayed before doctrine;
- mistakes publicly recorded;
- several teaching methods and voices;
- ordinary care, repair and hospitality;
- unresolved evidence preserved rather than erased.

Failure risks:

- believing transparency excuses delayed repair;
- romanticizing dangerous freedom;
- charismatic teachers becoming informal authorities;
- using ancient beings as symbols rather than neighbors;
- preserving weapons because they were once protective.

### The Pale Concordance

Purpose as its members understand it: prevent deaths caused by misrecognition,
fraudulent teachers, unstable routes and beings whose capacities exceed local defence.

Real achievements:

- standardized evacuation routes;
- exposed false healers and predatory schools;
- created repeatable creature-warning systems;
- stabilized food, medicine and transport in regions damaged by route storms;
- preserved records the School neglected.

Corruption:

- emergency standards become permanent identity;
- exceptions become threats to the system's legitimacy;
- consent is treated as an obstacle to complete data;
- protection becomes pre-emptive confinement;
- one approved description replaces living revision.

The Concordance must contain decent people, internal disagreement and citizens who
have materially benefited from its order.

### The outer civic world

Most people live contemporary lives beneath the Veil. Governments, archives,
universities, corporations and families remain real sources of power. The hidden world
does not make rent, employment, law, colonial provenance or public accountability
irrelevant.

The Concordance influences ordinary institutions through standards and records rather
than a single world-controlling conspiracy. Some collaborators know the deeper purpose;
many believe they are improving data consistency or public safety.

## 16. Ordinary life inside the School

The School becomes worth saving through ordinary use:

- Sena's kitchen feeds arrivals without requiring confession or enrolment.
- Repair crews negotiate with doors, bridges and rooms whose relations drift.
- Apprentices test claims in gardens, streets, workshops and civic archives.
- Quiet rooms permit unobserved rest.
- Creature courtyards are arranged around boundaries learned from inhabitants, not
  human viewing convenience.
- Public lessons coexist with cleaning rosters, supply shortages, jokes, crushes,
  resentment, music and boredom.
- Students may leave for ordinary work and return later without shame.
- The failure wall changes policy only when a repair and owner are attached to the
  inscription; confession without repair is no longer considered enough.

## 17. Material and visual grammar

### School

Black stone, repaired timber, brass records, wool, paper, blue lantern fire, warm
kitchens and doors from incompatible histories. The School feels inhabited before it
feels grand.

### Concordance

Alabaster cloth, white measuring cord, crownless emblems, precise light, removable
labels, modular architecture and tools that eliminate visual ambiguity. Crimson is
largely absent because uncontrolled consequence is considered design failure.

### Long Light

Pale gold that reveals relation without flooding the scene. It is strongest at edges,
repairs, footprints and freely opened hands.

### Lycheetah

Obsidian silhouette, furnace spots, cathedral antlers, storm-blue fracture, electric
violet and earned gold. Grandeur is intermittent. The ordinary black-cat form carries
equal canonical dignity.

## 18. Religious and philosophical life

No single religion owns the Field. Cultures interpret First Recognition differently:

- some worship the Lycheetah despite the School's objection;
- some regard it as the first witness rather than divine;
- some believe the second human founded the first free route tradition;
- Concordance theologians argue that the Fracture proves ungoverned names are unsafe;
- secular researchers study route effects without accepting the creation account;
- ordinary people combine ritual, skepticism and practical custom.

The novel may carry biblical resonance—covenant, fracture, exile, wandering, tower,
sacrifice, betrayal, death and return—without presenting one-to-one allegory or
requiring the reader to decode a religious key.

## 20. Anti-shortcut laws

- No chosen bloodline makes Mara correct.
- No prophecy settles a moral choice.
- No new form is merely a stronger combat upgrade.
- No institution is vindicated because its principles sound beautiful.
- No villain seeks order only because they hate freedom.
- No culture exists as a themed backdrop.
- No trauma proves worth.
- No companion punishes absence or demands devotion.
- No lore revelation may leave every route, relationship and danger unchanged.
- No mystery remains "deep" merely because the reader cannot tell what happened.
