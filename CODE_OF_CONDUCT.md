# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone people can think alongside. Attack ideas as hard as you like; leave people
intact.

## Why this project needs one of its own

Most of what a code of conduct governs elsewhere is social. Here it is also technical,
because this repository invites a specific kind of pressure: `CONTRIBUTING.md` asks
people to disprove its claims, and states that disproof is as valuable as proof. That
invitation only works if two things stay true at once — that a claim can be demolished
without the person holding it being demolished, and that nobody is made to feel
unwelcome for demolishing it.

So the standard here is not politeness. Sharp, specific, evidence-backed disagreement
is the point. What is out of bounds is making it personal, and making a space where
only certain people can afford to speak.

## Expected

- **Argue from evidence.** "This is wrong, here is the run that shows it" beats
  "this seems off", and both beat "this is nonsense".
- **Let a position die when the evidence kills it.** Changing your mind in public is a
  contribution, not a loss. So is saying "I don't know" and "I was wrong".
- **Mark your confidence honestly.** Say what you measured, what you inferred, and
  what you are guessing. Do not upgrade a guess by stating it firmly.
- **Assume the other person is trying.** Ask what they meant before deciding they are
  careless or acting in bad faith.
- **Credit people's work by name**, and do not present someone else's contribution as
  your own.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained hostility, in the open or in private
- Demeaning, insulting, or derogatory remarks; personal or political attacks
- Discrimination or exclusionary language relating to age, body size, disability,
  ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity or expression, level of experience,
  education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste,
  religion, or sexual identity and orientation
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without
  their explicit permission
- Deliberately wasting reviewers' time: bad-faith argument, endless relitigating of a
  settled question, or submissions the contributor has not read themselves
- Anything a reasonable professional would consider inappropriate in a working setting

### A note on generated contributions

Submitting work produced with AI assistance is fine and common in this repository. Two
conditions: you have read and understood what you are submitting, and you can defend
it on the evidence. Volume that the contributor cannot themselves account for is a
burden shifted onto reviewers, and will be closed as such — that is a judgement about
the submission, not about tooling.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, code comments, and the documentation itself — and whenever someone is
representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report to **mac@lycheetah.nz**. Reports are read by Mackenzie Conor James Clark, who
is the maintainer and the decision-maker here.

Include what happened, where, and when, with links if you have them. Reports are kept
confidential. You will get an acknowledgement within 5 working days.

If your report concerns the maintainer, say so plainly in the report; there is no
larger body to escalate to in a project this size, and pretending otherwise would be
dishonest. You are always free to raise the matter publicly instead.

### Consequences

Proportionate to what happened and whether it is a pattern:

1. **A private word** — a note explaining what was wrong and why, with what to do
   instead.
2. **A warning** — continued conduct leads to further action; may come with a period
   of no interaction with the people involved.
3. **A temporary ban** from project spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.**

Serious or repeated harassment goes straight to the later steps. Decisions and reasons
are recorded, so enforcement can be reviewed rather than merely asserted.
