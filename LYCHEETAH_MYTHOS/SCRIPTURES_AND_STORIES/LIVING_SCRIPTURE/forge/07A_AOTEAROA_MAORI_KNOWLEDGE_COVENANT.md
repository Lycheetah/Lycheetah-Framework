# 07A — AOTEAROA MĀORI KNOWLEDGE COVENANT
## Relationship and authority before transmutation

**Human author and final canon authority:** Mackenzie Conor James Clark  
**Co-author and research/narrative forge:** Vaelora · OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol  
**Status:** PROPOSED HARD BOUNDARY · v0.1 · 4 October 2026

This covenant exists because Māori knowledge in Aotearoa must not be handled as merely
another row in a comparative-philosophy table.

It is not a claim that every Māori concept is secret, restricted or incapable of public
study. It is a rule that **public visibility does not by itself settle authority, tikanga,
permission, context, ownership, responsibility, or appropriate reuse**.

## 1. No pan-Māori flattening

“Māori” is not one undifferentiated source voice.

Where a teaching, narrative, practice, place or taonga is connected to a particular iwi,
hapū, whānau, marae, mana whenua or knowledge holder, preserve that specificity.

Do not infer that permission, interpretation or tikanga from one context automatically
transfers to another.

## 2. Mātauranga is not raw material

Lycheetah may **study** public scholarship and public explanations of mātauranga Māori.
That does not automatically authorize Lycheetah to:

- convert mātauranga into proprietary framework primitives;
- rename it as a Lycheetah invention;
- turn whakapapa into a generic graph mechanic;
- extract pūrākau into decontextualized fantasy lore;
- manufacture Māori-sounding sacred language, karakia, waiata, atua systems or names;
- use tā moko, whakairo or other culturally meaningful forms as aesthetic texture;
- train, market or commercialize a Māori-derived system merely because the source was public.

## 3. Relationship gate

Before **substantive integration** of Māori knowledge into public Lycheetah scripture,
software, research, ritual, curriculum, naming, mythology or commercial work, identify:

1. What exactly is being used?
2. Where did it come from?
3. Is it place-, iwi-, hapū-, whānau- or holder-specific?
4. Who has standing to speak to appropriate use?
5. What tikanga applies?
6. What should remain untouched, private, tapu, restricted or context-bound?
7. What acknowledgement, control, access, protection or benefit-sharing is appropriate?
8. Can the relevant people say **no** or later ask for revision/removal?

If those questions cannot yet be answered, the default state is:

> **STUDY / CITE / DO NOT TRANSMUTE.**

## 4. Mana whenua gate

Place-based Lycheetah work in Aotearoa must not treat “Māori tradition” as geographically
interchangeable.

When material is connected to a place, identify the relevant mana whenua and local
context before turning that material into story-world canon, ritual or curriculum.

## 5. Authority gate

An AI system, including Vaelora, may assist with:

- source discovery;
- comparison;
- transcription and organization;
- provenance tracking;
- drafting questions for human review.

It may not appoint itself a Māori cultural authority, determine tikanga on behalf of
Māori, grant permission, or convert lack of objection into consent.

## 6. Taonga and restricted-material gate

Some knowledge, expressions, biological relationships, names, genealogies, practices or
cultural works may carry kaitiaki interests, restrictions or obligations not visible to
an outsider.

Therefore:

> **Accessible does not mean available for any use.**

Where uncertainty exists, seek the relevant human authority before transmutation.

## 7. Provenance is necessary but insufficient

A citation can show where Lycheetah found something.

A citation cannot by itself answer:

- whether the reuse is appropriate;
- whether the right people were involved;
- whether a teaching has been stripped from tikanga;
- whether commercial benefit should be shared;
- whether a public rendering should exist at all.

## 8. No proprietary enclosure

Lycheetah must not use a Māori-derived concept, form, story, term or relationship as a
basis for claiming exclusive ownership over the underlying Māori knowledge.

If an original Lycheetah work is created after legitimate collaboration, the distinct
contributions and agreed rights must remain legible.

## 9. Commercialization raises the gate

Commercial use requires **more** care than private study, not less.

Before commercializing material substantially dependent on mātauranga Māori, establish
agreed terms with relevant people, including where appropriate:

- attribution;
- governance or approval;
- access restrictions;
- protection;
- benefit-sharing;
- revision/removal processes.

## 10. Correction and withdrawal

If relevant Māori knowledge holders identify a misuse, false generalization, broken
context or harmful transmutation, Lycheetah must be able to:

- correct the claim;
- revise the work;
- remove the material;
- preserve an internal provenance record of why the correction occurred.

The project's desire for continuity does not outrank the source relationship.

## 11. Relationship is not a checkbox

One consultation does not permanently authorize an entire domain.

Human relationship, tikanga and accountability continue as the work changes.

## 12. Current Lycheetah rule

For the present Living Scripture forge:

- Māori sources may inform **research notes and provenance-aware comparison**.
- No Māori teaching is yet declared a Lycheetah sacred doctrine.
- No mātauranga Māori is yet transmuted into proprietary canon.
- `Whakapapa` remains a named Māori concept, not a CASCADE/LAMAGUE primitive.
- Any future substantive Māori-derived scripture or School curriculum remains
  **BLOCKED_PENDING_RELEVANT_MAORI_HUMAN_RELATIONSHIP_AND_REVIEW**.

## Grounding used for this covenant

This project boundary is informed by, but does not claim to reproduce or replace:

- the Waitangi Tribunal's Wai 262 / *Ko Aotearoa Tēnei* discussion of tino
  rangatiratanga, kaitiaki relationships, Māori culture, identity and traditional
  knowledge;
- Te Mana Raraunga's Māori Data Sovereignty principles concerning rangatiratanga,
  whakapapa, whanaungatanga, manaakitanga and kaitiakitanga in data contexts;
- current MBIE IP-management guidance stating that use of mātauranga Māori should be
  subject to agreed terms with relevant persons, including access, protection and
  benefit-sharing where appropriate;
- Royal Society Te Apārangi's Research Charter expectations to identify and engage
  affected people and communities and, where possible, collaborate with mana whenua,
  tangata whenua, iwi and hapū directly affected by research.

These sources are **guidance and accountability references**. They do not grant Lycheetah
permission to use any particular mātauranga Māori.

## Closing law

> **In Aotearoa, relationship precedes transmutation.  
> Provenance names the source. Tikanga helps determine the right use.  
> Neither the School nor the machine gets to appoint itself kaitiaki.**
