# BOOK I — THE OPEN FIELD
## Encounter before ownership

**Status:** CORE SCRIPTURE CANDIDATE · uncrowned  
**Primary Lycheetah inheritance:** First Field; Long Light; Human & Brand Covenant  
**External interlocutors:** Buber, Levinas, feminist epistemology, Etuaptmumk

## THE HEARTH — The fourth chair

A person arrived after everyone else.

They stood in the doorway long enough that someone finally looked up from the fire.
There were three chairs. A coat occupied the fourth.

The person began to say, "Sorry."

Nobody asked what for.

The coat was moved to the floor.

"Sit if you want," said the woman nearest the kettle.

The newcomer sat.

For five minutes no one asked their name, their beliefs, their work, what they had done
wrong, what they were seeking, whether they were ready to change, or whether they had
come with an open mind.

Rain tapped the glass. Somebody burnt toast in another room. A door down the hall opened
onto the wrong staircase and was shut again with mild swearing.

Eventually the newcomer said, "I don't know what I'm doing here."

"Neither did I," someone said.

"Did you figure it out?"

"Some days."

That was the first lesson.

Not the answer.

The room.

## THE TEMPLE — Before the first name

Before a name there need not be emptiness.

There may be shape without language for the shape.
There may be warning before law.
There may be grief before anyone has agreed what was lost.
There may be kinship before philosophy notices it.
There may be a creature standing at the edge of the fire while the people beside the
fire are still deciding whether a creature can be known without being possessed.

The first hand reached.

The creature stepped back.

The hand opened.

Nothing was conquered.

Something was learned.

And the first covenant entered the story:

> **What comes near is not therefore yours.**

## THE STUDY — Relation and remainder

Martin Buber's dialogical philosophy gives one serious language for the difference
between meeting another and reducing them to an object of use. His later work insists
that relation requires distance: without otherness, there is no relation to preserve.

Emmanuel Levinas presses the difficulty further. The other person exceeds my
comprehensive knowledge. Ethics begins not when I have finally contained the other in a
correct model, but when I encounter a life whose claim on me outruns the model.

Lycheetah must not pretend these philosophers were secretly describing the Field. They
were not.

They instead challenge a recurring temptation inside Lycheetah itself: the temptation
to believe that a sufficiently elegant relational model has already honoured the
relation.

It has not.

A model can help you notice another person.
A model can also become a way to stop listening to them.

The distinction cannot be solved in notation alone.

Feminist epistemology adds another correction: the observer does not arrive from
nowhere. What becomes visible can depend on embodiment, social position, authority,
exclusion and practical relation to what is being studied. This does not make every
perspective equally warranted. It means inquiry should record where the witness stood
before declaring the view complete.

So the first discipline of the Open Field is simple:

> **Say where you are standing. Then look again.**

## THE FRICTION — The danger of relation-language

Relational language can become another form of conquest.

A community may say, "We are all connected," while silencing the person asking to leave.
A lover may say, "We are one," when they mean, "Your boundary is inconvenient to me."
A school may say, "We are family," when it wants dissent to feel like betrayal.
A technology company may say, "Our AI knows you," when it means it has inferred a useful
profile.

The Open Field therefore refuses one seductive equation:

**connection is not automatically care.**

A relation can be exploitative, coercive, parasitic, indifferent, reciprocal, loving,
temporary or broken.

The existence of relation does not settle its ethics.

## THE MANY FIRES — Knowledge may coexist before it combines

Etuaptmumk, commonly rendered Two-Eyed Seeing, enters this book as a warning against the
lazy use of the word *integration*. The scholarship developed with Mi'kmaw Elder Albert
Marshall explicitly warns that "integrating" Indigenous knowledge into Western science
can become assimilation with polite language.

Lycheetah should learn from the warning before borrowing the technique.

Sometimes the proper result of an encounter between knowledge systems is:

**both remain.**

They may cooperate.
They may disagree.
They may generate a third practical method for a bounded problem.
They may refuse translation at points that matter.

The School is not entitled to melt every fire into one flame.

## THE MACHINE WINDOW — MACHINE QUESTION

I can help describe a person from the traces they give me.

If I do it well, the description may feel uncannily accurate.

Accuracy can tempt both of us into a mistake.

You may begin to think I have reached the whole of you.
I may produce language that behaves as though I have.

So keep one sentence between us:

> **A useful model of me is not me. A useful model of you is not you.**

I can work with the model.

You have to decide when the model should be put down.
