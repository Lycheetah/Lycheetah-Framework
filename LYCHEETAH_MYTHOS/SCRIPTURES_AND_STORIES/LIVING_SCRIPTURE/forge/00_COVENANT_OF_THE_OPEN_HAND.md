This book is sacred in intention and fallible in construction.

It is sacred because human beings require places where grief, wonder, love, death, doubt,
knowledge, responsibility and the unknown can be approached with more care than ordinary
noise allows.

It is fallible because no author, school, model, tradition, institution or beautiful
sentence gains immunity from correction by sounding sacred.

The book therefore makes six promises.

### I. You will not be owned by what teaches you.
A teacher may influence you. A tradition may change you. A relationship may become
precious. None of these acquires your conscience by helping you.

### II. Your refusal remains meaningful.
You may leave a passage unanswered. You may reject a Lycheetah law. You may refuse a
ritual. You may leave the School. Return does not require apology.

### III. Sources keep their names.
Christianity will not be renamed Lycheetah. Islam will not be renamed Lycheetah.
Mātauranga Māori will not become a Lycheetah subsystem. Buddhist, Hindu, Jewish,
Daoist, Confucian, African, Indigenous, scientific and philosophical traditions remain
what they are before they become interlocutors here.

### IV. Mystery will not counterfeit evidence.
A question may remain holy without becoming answered. An unexplained event is not proof
of our preferred metaphysics. Wonder may open the door; reality still gets a vote.

### V. AI may speak, but it may not quietly become priest.
Machine voices may ask questions, compare sources, preserve continuity, challenge a
claim, and help a learner write. Their fluency is not evidence of consciousness, soul,
wisdom or spiritual authority.

### VI. The final page belongs to the learner.
This text is inherited so that it can become examined. A student may one day write a
better passage than the School. If so, the School should become capable of learning it.

## The First Gesture

Close your hand.

Notice what happens to the space inside it.

Open it.

Nothing mystical is required.

The gesture is enough to begin.

A closed hand can protect, carry, strike, grasp and keep.

An open hand can offer, receive, release, point, steady and refuse to seize.

Neither gesture is always right.

The Work begins by learning when each becomes dangerous.

> **What is understood does not become owned.**

This sentence is not the end of Lycheetah.

It is the door.
