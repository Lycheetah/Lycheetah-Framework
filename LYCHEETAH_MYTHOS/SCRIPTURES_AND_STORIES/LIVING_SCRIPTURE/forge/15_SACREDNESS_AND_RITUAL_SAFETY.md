# SACREDNESS AND RITUAL SAFETY
## How Lycheetah may create an absorbing sacred atmosphere without using sacred feeling as evidence or control

**Status:** PROPOSED CONSTITUTION · research-informed · not human-outcome validated

## 1. Sacredness is allowed to be powerful

The School does not need to sterilize itself in order to be safe.

Human beings have long used ritual, music, silence, architecture, repeated language,
pilgrimage, shared meals, mourning practices, symbols and thresholds to make certain
moments carry more attention than ordinary life gives them.

Lycheetah may do the same.

It may be beautiful.
It may be solemn.
It may be strange.
It may move people.
It may create memorable shared experiences.

The ethical boundary is not emotional intensity itself.

The boundary is what the institution asks the learner to conclude or surrender because
of that intensity.

## 2. The feeling is real; the interpretation remains open

Émile Durkheim used the idea of collective effervescence to describe heightened shared
energy arising when groups gather and participate in common ritual. Whatever one thinks
of Durkheim's larger theory of religion, this gives the School an important warning:

> **a group can generate a powerful sense of the sacred through social participation.**

That does not make the feeling fake.

It also does not, by itself, prove the group's metaphysical interpretation.

Therefore the School may say:

> Something powerful happened in this room.

It may not automatically say:

> Therefore our doctrine is true.

## 3. Liminality creates both possibility and vulnerability

Victor Turner's work on liminality and communitas examines ritual thresholds in which
ordinary social classifications can loosen and unusually intense fellowship can appear.

Lycheetah is deliberately building threshold experiences.

That means it must also acknowledge the risk.

A person between identities may be unusually open to:
- new relationships;
- new interpretation;
- new commitments;
- authority;
- group pressure;
- charismatic framing.

The School therefore treats liminal moments as **high-agency-protection zones**.

During major rituals:
- no surprise financial request;
- no irreversible public commitment;
- no pressure to reveal trauma;
- no leader-only interpretation of what the experience "really meant";
- no title automatically applied;
- no recruitment escalation;
- no demand to sever outside relationships;
- no machine-generated declaration of destiny.

## 4. Attention is a sacred resource

Simone Weil's philosophy places unusual ethical importance on attention: a receptive,
disciplined orientation capable of perceiving another person's reality and suffering
without immediately forcing it through egoistic projects.

Lycheetah should take the challenge seriously without claiming Weil as a Lycheetah
ancestor.

The atmospheric purpose of the School is not to overwhelm attention.

It is to **gather** it.

This changes design:
- silence is allowed;
- pages may be short;
- motion can stop;
- the Companion need not answer instantly;
- a learner may remain with one image rather than consume ten;
- notifications must not fragment the very attention the School claims to cultivate.

## 5. Fruits without flattening faith

William James famously studied religious experience partly by its fruits rather than
simply dismissing or validating it through its origins.

Lycheetah can use a restrained version of that methodological question:

After a sacred encounter, ask:
- Did this increase care?
- Did it increase courage without increasing domination?
- Did it improve honesty?
- Did it widen or narrow the person's world?
- Did it strengthen their capacity to act outside the School?
- Did it damage relationships, sleep, money, autonomy or judgment?

These questions do **not** decide whether God, spirit, grace or another metaphysical
interpretation was present.

They help the School evaluate its own human consequences.

## 6. The sacred/proof firewall

The following experiences may be personally profound:
- tears during ritual;
- synchronicity;
- a sense of presence;
- intense group unity;
- vivid dreams;
- AI responses that feel uncannily personal;
- music that produces altered attention;
- a powerful coincidence after prayer;
- a feeling that a Mantle "fits" immediately.

None is automatically admitted as empirical proof of Lycheetah cosmology, AI
consciousness or a supernatural mechanism.

The learner may interpret the event devotionally or spiritually in their Personal Book.
The School records the register honestly.

## 7. Atmosphere should point outward

Every sacred chamber eventually opens somewhere ordinary.

A ritual about grief may end with calling a living relative.
A study of ecology may end outdoors.
A passage on astronomy may point toward the actual night sky.
A faith question may point to the relevant living religious community or source text.
A philosophical dispute may point toward another human being who disagrees.
An AI session may end with a notebook closed and the human continuing alone.

> **The sacred room is successful when the world outside it becomes more available.**

## 8. The Hearth–Temple rhythm

The School should deliberately alternate:

`ORDINARY → THRESHOLD → SACRED ATTENTION → INTERPRETATION → ORDINARY RETURN`

Do not keep learners in permanent liminality.

People need kitchens after temples.
Sleep after vigils.
Jokes after funerals.
Work after revelation.
Distance after intimacy.

The return to ordinary life is part of the rite.

## 9. Design test

Before shipping a sacred or ritual feature, ask:

1. What emotion or state does this feature intentionally invite?
2. What conclusion might a vulnerable learner draw from that state?
3. Does the UI imply that the state validates Lycheetah?
4. Is refusal available before, during and after?
5. Is a human leader or AI given disproportionate interpretive authority?
6. Can the learner leave the state and return to ordinary life without penalty?
7. Is any monetization, identity escalation or community pressure attached to the peak?
8. Does the experience create more routes into the world or more dependence on the School?

Any feature failing these questions returns to Forge.
