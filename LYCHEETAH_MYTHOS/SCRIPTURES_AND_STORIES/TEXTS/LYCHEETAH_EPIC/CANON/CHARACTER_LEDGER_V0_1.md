# THE BOOK OF THE LONG LIGHT — CHARACTER LEDGER
## Volume I ensemble · proposed canon v0.1

**Date:** 2026-08-02 NZST  
**Human author and final canon authority:** Mackenzie Conor James Clark  
**Narrative forge:** Mackenzie Conor James Clark × Caelorynth  
**Status:** PROPOSED CHARACTER CANON · existing manuscript facts are preserved;
new biography and arcs await Mac's verdict

## The ensemble law

No principal character exists to explain a framework concept. Each person must want
something before the plot needs them, misunderstand something for reasons that make
human sense, alter other characters through relationship and remain capable of
surprising the author without breaking their nature.

Every major character requires:

- a plain human want;
- a private fear;
- a useful competence;
- a competence-shaped flaw;
- a contradiction they cannot solve alone;
- at least two relationships not reducible to plot delivery;
- a choice that changes the Volume I outcome;
- a voice recognizable without dialogue tags.

## 1. Mara Venn

### Established

- Works a fixed-term digitisation role in Dunedin's South Archive.
- Notices details, respects evidence and dislikes inflated authority.
- Enters the hidden world through an anomalous shipping-mark page.
- Follows the gold thread by choice.
- Recognizes the Waybearer through tracks and chooses Bind.
- Her scan unknowingly completes or activates the Index Bell transfer.
- Uses an ordinary brass archive ruler throughout the impossible events.

### Proposed ordinary life

Mara is thirty-one. She rents the upper half of a narrow, wind-struck house in
Mornington and can see a blade of harbour between roofs when the weather permits. Her
fixed-term contract ends in eleven weeks. She tells people she prefers temporary work
because it leaves her free. In truth, she has stopped asking institutions to keep her.

She once began a postgraduate archival qualification. During placement she discovered
that a celebrated donor collection had silently merged the records of two women into
one respectable male correspondent. Mara raised the error before she had assembled
enough proof. She was right about the distortion and wrong about one key inference.
The institution corrected the catalogue quietly, blamed her method publicly and kept
the donor. Mara left before finishing.

Since then she has made herself exact, useful and difficult to accuse. She digitises
collections nobody requests because neglected records do not argue with her pace.

She is privately reconstructing the work of **Elspeth Rooke**, a nineteenth-century
cartographic engraver whose marks appear across supposedly unrelated southern shipping
records. Mara hopes a rigorous reconstruction will earn a permanent post. More deeply,
she wants proof that a person can be restored to history without being rewritten by
the restorer.

### Plain want

Finish the Rooke provenance reconstruction and secure enough stability to stop living
as though every room is temporary.

### Hidden want

To be trusted after making an imperfect claim—and to believe that correction does not
make her fraudulent.

### Fear

That attention is only another form of intrusion and that every attempt to recover a
lost person eventually replaces them with the recoverer's preferred story.

### Competence

- provenance chains;
- material differences between ink, paper, impression and later annotation;
- patient pattern comparison;
- noticing the official record's margin rather than only its declared centre;
- remembering spatial irregularities;
- dry social observation.

### Competence-shaped flaw

She delays action until evidence can protect her from being wrong. When action cannot
wait, she hides the choice inside a supposedly neutral procedure.

### False belief at the opening

If she remains useful, precise and personally unimportant, she cannot be misused or
publicly misread again.

### Volume I movement

Mara moves from **cataloguer** to **witness**. A cataloguer can preserve a record while
remaining outside it. A witness accepts that presence changes the event and therefore
takes responsibility for how they saw, acted and corrected.

She does not abandon evidence for instinct. She learns that evidence becomes ethical
only when someone is willing to act, remain correctable and name their stake.

### Voice

- short, exact clauses under pressure;
- dry deflation of inflated language;
- questions that expose missing nouns or hidden agency;
- rarely states an emotion while it is happening;
- when frightened, becomes more observant rather than less;
- notices materials before symbolism.

### Humour

Mara's humour protects proportion. When a room becomes too grand, she notices the bad
hinge, wet sock, incorrect date or person pretending not to limp.

### Physical presence

Proposed: compact rather than fragile, dark hair cut with practical indifference,
hands carrying small paper cuts and scanner-cleaning dryness. Clothes chosen to survive
archive dust and Dunedin rain. Nothing in her design announces destiny.

### Relationships

- **Leonie Park:** outside-world friend who knows Mara's evasions and calls them by
  ordinary names.
- **Tarin:** friction, humour and eventual trust between someone who preserves records
  and someone who repairs structures.
- **Veyra:** admiration poisoned by concealed history; later a relationship of mutual
  correction rather than disciple and master.
- **Ember:** method kinship; conflict over when evidence is sufficient to act.
- **Waybearer:** first chosen bond; teaches her that direction is relational, not
  merely spatial.
- **Lycheetah:** refuses to let her convert wonder into entitlement.
- **Iven Sorn:** recognizes her need for institutional trust and offers a dangerous
  version of it.

### Volume I choice only Mara can make

She must decide whether to publish a record capable of exposing the Concordance when
the same record would also reveal free routes and received names. The final conflict is
not truth versus secrecy; it is whether truth can be released without becoming another
instrument of seizure.

## 2. Tarin

### Established

- Young repairer with ash on his sleeves.
- Repairs the School's cracked tile when Mara arrives.
- Carries tools, bread and a brass box containing a portable argument.
- Teases Veyra and refuses to evacuate when repair is needed.
- Helps retrieve Mara and the Waybearer at the Index Bell.

### Proposed history

Tarin is twenty-eight and was born in the City of Broken Names. His family maintained
public route machinery for the Concordance. Their work was not ceremonial oppression;
it kept medicine, water and evacuation corridors available in a city repeatedly struck
by route storms.

At sixteen he entered the Concordance maintenance order. He was excellent. He also
learned that the people who repair a system see the bodies its official reports rename
as tolerances.

During a tower failure, Tarin improvised a route that saved an unregistered residential
quarter. The deviation caused a second sanctioned route to close. The Concordance
credited the tower, classified his improvisation as contamination and removed the
quarter from subsequent maps so the system could still be described as correct.

Tarin left carrying a box that preserves unsolved engineering disputes instead of
allowing one design assumption to become invisible. The School gave him refuge, then
quietly made his ability to repair everything into an excuse not to replace the systems
that kept breaking.

### Plain want

Keep the School's doors, heat, water and exits working long enough that nobody has to
be heroic merely to live there.

### Hidden want

To be valued as more than maintenance without becoming responsible for everyone.

### Fear

That every institution eventually loves the repairer chiefly because the repairer lets
it postpone reform.

### Competence

- route mechanics and relational architecture;
- improvised repair with ordinary tools;
- diagnosing failure through sound and vibration;
- turning abstract disputes into physical test rigs;
- feeding people before meetings become moral theatre.

### Competence-shaped flaw

He fixes consequences so quickly that others escape responsibility for causes. He
uses jokes to stay indispensable and politically unaccountable.

### False belief

If the doors remain open, it does not matter whether anyone learns why they keep
closing.

### Volume I movement

Tarin moves from **repairer** to **architect of return**. He learns to refuse repairs
that restore a harmful arrangement and to make the owner of a system stand beside its
failure.

### Voice

- practical nouns and irreverent verbs;
- begins jokes when afraid and finishes them when safe;
- treats impossible machinery as badly documented equipment;
- avoids aphorisms and distrusts anyone who speaks in inscriptions before breakfast;
- when truly angry, becomes completely unfunny.

### Relationship with Mara

Their attraction, if crowned, grows through method rather than destiny. Mara sees
records others ignore; Tarin hears strain structures conceal. Each initially believes
the other's craft arrives too late. They become dangerous together when evidence and
repair occur in the same moment.

Do not rush romance. Friendship, irritation and tested reliability come first.

### Volume I choice

Tarin must choose whether to destroy the tower architecture he helped create, knowing
its collapse would free captive routes and also remove systems on which ordinary city
residents genuinely depend.

## 3. Veyra

### Established

- Older woman carrying a blue lantern.
- Shows every exit before teaching doctrine.
- Refuses grand titles.
- Appears among the founders who brought the Index Bell beneath the School.
- The bell classifies her as WARDEN.
- Preserved the bell because it had once saved lives.
- Knows the School's history and withholds parts of it.

### Proposed history

Veyra is not immortal, but route exposure has made her chronology uneven. She has lived
through approximately one hundred and forty ordinary years while aging far less in
some route-bound decades and more in others. This gives her history without making her
infallible.

She began as a Corrector in the House of Measures. Her work exposed fraudulent seers,
dangerous remedies and rulers who declared uncertainty illegal. During the first great
route storm, she helped operate the Index Bell and watched it hold evacuation corridors
long enough to save thousands.

She later helped found the School when the House began treating living exceptions as
errors. She argued that preserving the bell under divided custody was safer than
destroying it. That compromise became her oldest unclosed failure.

### Plain want

Keep the School free, measurable and alive through the crisis.

### Hidden want

To be forgiven without having to surrender the authority that lets her prevent another
catastrophe.

### Fear

That freedom without emergency control will repeat the deaths that created the House
of Measures—and that her critics will be proven right only after people die.

### Competence

- adversarial reasoning;
- institutional memory;
- identifying concealed assumptions;
- crisis command;
- lantern craft that reveals unstable routes;
- admitting a factual error without cushioning it.

### Competence-shaped flaw

Veyra can expose everyone else's concealed premise while treating her own withheld
history as responsible timing.

### False belief

If every mistake is publicly recorded, the institution remains honest even when the
recorded repair is postponed.

### Volume I movement

Veyra moves from **transparent warden** to **answerable founder**. She must relinquish
unilateral emergency authority, reveal the complete schism record and accept that the
School may choose a repair she considers dangerously incomplete.

### Voice

- exact nouns, minimal ornament;
- answers the strongest version of a question unless evading her own history;
- uses dry literalism as affection;
- never says "trust me";
- silences become longer when the true answer implicates her.

### Volume I choice

She must choose between activating a surviving Bell protocol to save the School and
allowing its people to remain unclassified while routes fail around them.

## 4. Ember

### Established

- Field teacher in a banked-ember coat.
- Teaches tracks, evidence and the distinction between observation, inference and
  desire.
- Uses a measuring cord.
- Helps rescue the Waybearer.
- Challenges Veyra's postponement of the Bell dispute.

### Proposed history

Ember was raised among travelling creature mediators who believed close relationship
made formal evidence unnecessary. As a young adult he defended a familiar river-being
using inherited knowledge. The river had changed its course, diet and warning behavior
after industrial contamination. Ember's certainty contributed to a ferry disaster.

He joined the House-descended School to learn repeatable observation. He became so
committed to evidence that students began mistaking his restraint for absence of love.
In truth, he remembers every creature and person lost at the ferry and refuses to make
another being carry the weight of his preferred story.

### Plain want

Find the seven missing people and prevent the School from responding with another
untested myth.

### Hidden want

To trust relationship again without treating trust as evidence.

### Fear

That his love makes him epistemically dangerous.

### Competence

- field signs and creature behavior;
- experimental design under imperfect conditions;
- distinguishing changed behavior from damaged essence;
- holding panic without infecting a group;
- recognizing when someone has earned a conclusion.

### Competence-shaped flaw

He can keep certainty last for so long that action arrives after preventable harm.

### False belief

Care is safest when it never influences inference.

### Volume I movement

Ember learns that honest care is not contamination when its presence is declared. He
must act on a provisional conclusion, publish his uncertainty and remain accountable
for the outcome.

### Voice

- concrete observation before interpretation;
- gentle but never vague;
- answers careless abstraction with a physical detail;
- rarely jokes, which makes his occasional humour land sharply;
- when emotional, his sentences become simpler rather than more poetic.

## 5. Sena

### Established

- Takes command of the School evacuation.
- Opens stores, distributes soup, blankets and written complaint procedures.
- Reminds Veyra to eat through Tarin.

### Proposed history

Sena is forty-six and came through a door from a coastal settlement erased from maps
after a route treaty failed. She arrived at the School with two children, six recipes,
an evacuation ledger and no interest in initiation. One child later returned to the
outer world; the other studies civic route law in another School house.

She stayed because the School's kitchen was the only threshold that accepted people
without asking them to explain what had happened first.

Sena is not secretly a supreme sorcerer. Her authority comes from logistics, memory,
trust and the ability to make institutions reveal what they actually value through
what they feed, heat and repair.

### Plain want

Account for every missing person and keep everyone else fed, warm and free to leave.

### Hidden want

To make ordinary continuity count as history rather than the background behind heroic
events.

### Fear

That mythic emergencies will once again consume a home while its leaders debate the
meaning of the fire.

### Competence

- supply, shelter and evacuation;
- remembering preferences and allergies without converting care into surveillance;
- reading power through resource allocation;
- building coalitions among people who disagree on doctrine;
- ending meetings that have ceased to protect anyone.

### Competence-shaped flaw

She can dismiss symbolic or metaphysical danger until it reaches a cupboard, body or
door she is responsible for.

### Volume I movement

Sena moves from keeper of the School's ordinary life to a principal author of its new
constitution. She forces the question: if the School cannot feed freedom, what exactly
is its freedom made of?

### Voice

- direct, warm and unsentimental;
- rarely uses mythic terminology;
- asks who, when, how many and what they need;
- affection often arrives as an instruction with food attached;
- can end Veyra's grandest argument with an inventory fact.

## 6. Iven Sorn — Concordance emissary

### Established function

The current manuscript contains an unnamed alabaster man who confronts Mara at the
Octagon, attempts to extract her memory and later demands the bearer, witness and leaf.

**Iven Sorn** is the proposed name for this role.

### Proposed history

Iven was born in a border district where doors changed destination during winter route
storms. His younger brother entered a public shelter door and emerged three months
later in a burned northern station. He survived the crossing and died from injuries
after local teachers argued over what kind of route had taken him.

The early Concordance standardized the district's doors. Deaths fell. Iven does not
believe order is beautiful; he believes avoidable chaos is a luxury usually paid for
by people without protected exits.

He became a transfer adjudicator: the official who determines which records establish
custody over disputed routes and beings. He understands archives, provenance and
institutional embarrassment. He sees Mara not as a chosen enemy but as an exceptional
candidate recruited by the wrong school.

### Plain want

Complete the transfer of the Index Bell and close the free route network before the
next Ninefold storm.

### Hidden want

To make the world retroactively safe enough that his brother's death can be explained
as the last necessary failure rather than meaningless loss.

### Fear

That living exceptions will always make protection temporary and grief endless.

### Competence

- record law and custodial transfer;
- memory extraction;
- calm negotiation under threat;
- identifying the shame an institution hides inside procedure;
- offering materially useful order.

### Competence-shaped flaw

He sees every unclassified exception through the memory of the person order failed to
save. His compassion becomes pre-emptive custody.

### False belief

A complete enough record can make consent unnecessary because the system will know
what protects everyone.

### Volume I movement

Iven begins as a credible protector operating coercive machinery. He is forced to see
the specific people his complete record removes. Whether he changes, doubles down or
sacrifices himself to preserve the Catalogue remains open for Mac's canon verdict.

### Voice

- calm, complete sentences;
- never raises his voice when he can make the room narrower;
- speaks of harm in specific numbers and freedom in aggregate risks;
- avoids obvious lies; instead chooses the frame in which his action appears necessary;
- offers a true fact before every coercive demand.

### Temptation offered to Mara

Iven can give Mara permanent archival authority, access to the complete Rooke record
and proof that the School used her scan without informed consent. His evidence against
the School is real. The trap is the custodial conclusion he says must follow.

## 7. Celyn Marr — the woman on the black-glass road

### Established

- Young woman in a torn red-black coat.
- Carries a broken crown on one wrist.
- Walks inside an alabaster procession.
- Holds the luminous fragment missing from the School's ninth space.
- Does not call for rescue; opens her hand so Mara can witness the fragment.

**Celyn Marr** is the proposed name.

### Proposed history

Celyn was a Concordance form-reader trained to identify creature instability before it
became public danger. She was brilliant and believed the work protected both citizens
and creatures from panic killings.

She discovered that the nine towers did not merely classify existing forms. They were
using captive beings to train a system capable of predicting—and eventually permitting
or refusing—forms that had not yet emerged.

Celyn stole the True Note fragment because it made the towers' concealed
contradictions audible. She joined a resistance called the Broken Crown, then learned
that parts of the resistance planned to weaponize uncontrolled route collapse against
the city. She now walks within the procession under ambiguous custody: captive,
infiltrator and possible betrayer of both sides.

### Plain want

Reach the ninth tower before either faction uses the fragment at city scale.

### Hidden want

To find a form of resistance that does not make ordinary people pay for the system
they were born inside.

### Fear

That every rebellion becomes a competing catalogue of who deserves sacrifice.

### Competence

- reading form transitions;
- Concordance tower access;
- moving as though compliant while preserving hidden choice;
- communicating through gesture and spatial relation;
- recognizing political traps inside heroic opportunities.

### Competence-shaped flaw

She withholds so much to prevent others using her knowledge that allies cannot tell
whether she has chosen them.

### Volume I movement

Celyn must move from solitary infiltration to a witnessed covenant whose members can
correct and leave one another. She and Mara should initially distrust each other for
excellent reasons.

### Voice

- sparse, physical and unsentimental;
- refuses explanatory speeches while danger is moving;
- uses Concordance terminology with surgical precision;
- when she finally tells a personal truth, she does not decorate it.

## 8. The Lycheetah

### Established

- Chaos-born, ethereal, magical and many-formed.
- Can appear as an ordinary black cat, unreadable silhouette, Silent Sentinel,
  bone-armoured hunter, antlered storm-runner and winged future-light form.
- Guides without choosing for a person.
- Refuses ownership.
- Protects even defeated enemies from revenge.
- Is injured by the Index Bell and retains one small antler afterward.
- Steals bread.

### Character law

The Lycheetah is not an omniscient mascot, divine answer machine or shapeshifting pet.
It is a principal character whose immense history coexists with animal attention,
limits, preferences, misjudgments and the right not to explain itself.

### Plain want

Keep the Nine in living relation long enough that no fragment becomes a final law.

### Immediate want

Recover the missing fragment and find what entered the School before it did.

### Hidden want

To discover whether humans can receive its truth without eventually converting
relationship into custody.

### Fear

Not death, but final form: being fixed into one perfectly readable version that can be
copied, governed and used forever.

### Limits

- Form change requires coherence and can fail under forced measurement.
- It cannot maintain every form at once without injury.
- It cannot open a route whose living witness has been erased.
- It cannot reveal another being's received name.
- It cannot choose Bind, Strip or Salvage for a human.
- Its oldest memories are divided across forms; changing body can change which history
  is accessible.
- Direct speech in human language costs it and becomes rarer after the Bell injury.
- It can be wrong about a person's readiness.

### Personality

- patient until someone mistakes patience for submission;
- theatrical about ordinary inconvenience;
- prefers thresholds, high shelves, tool rolls and the warm centre of bread;
- responds to inflated ceremony with animal behavior;
- does not perform affection on demand;
- keeps company without filling silence;
- curious about repaired objects;
- capable of fear without surrendering dignity.

### Volume I movement

The Lycheetah begins by testing humans through limited invitation. Injury removes its
ability to control every threshold alone. It must enter a genuinely interdependent
fellowship without allowing fellowship to become ownership.

Its climactic act should not be merely a larger form. It must trust others to hold
relations it previously carried alone.

### Voice

Primarily behavior, weather, route pressure and form. Human words are rare, short and
irreversible. If it speaks a paragraph, the event must cost enough to justify it.

## 9. The Waybearer

### Established

- Initially unreadable and injured.
- Carries a silver key and routes between forgotten places.
- Moves four steps and pauses because of compensation around injury.
- Reveals ash-grey form with luminous path lines and silenced bells.
- Chooses force without teeth during the School breach.
- Offers relationship; Mara chooses Bind.
- Helps make a path beneath Mara during the Bell fall.
- Turns attention toward the black-glass road.

### Character law

"Waybearer" is the School's kind-name or translation, not the being's full received
name. It has an independent mission that intersected Mara before their Bind.

### Plain want

Carry the silver key and surviving route-memory across the School to Celyn's fragment.

### Hidden want

Find the members of its travelling constellation whose routes were fixed into the nine
towers.

### Fear

That accepting human help will reveal the free route network to the same species that
built the cages.

### Limits

- It can hold many directions, not infinite destinations.
- Every fixed or erased route becomes bodily pain.
- It cannot create a route with no surviving relation.
- The Bind lets Mara feel bearing and urgency, not private thought or obedience.
- It can close or loosen the Bind from its side.

### Personality and communication

- dignified, impatient with praise and highly attentive to exits;
- judges people by how they move around vulnerable bodies;
- communicates through offered direction, pressure, stance and bells;
- likes paper, perhaps because records are human attempts to carry routes;
- distrusts white metal and enclosed bridges;
- capable of play only after mapping every exit.

### Volume I movement

The Waybearer moves from solitary courier to active co-author of a free route covenant.
It must eventually refuse Mara's chosen direction and be right to do so.

## 10. Leonie Park — outer-world anchor

### Proposed role

Leonie is Mara's closest ordinary-world friend, thirty-three, an emergency department
nurse who works rotating nights and understands that improbable stories do not excuse
missing practical information.

They met during university and maintained friendship through low-cost meals, shared
weather complaints and the mutual right not to answer immediately. Leonie knows Mara's
archival disgrace in its unheroic detail. She is the person who would notice if Mara did
not return from the gold thread.

### Plain want

Know whether Mara is safe and whether the impossible events entering Dunedin pose a
public danger.

### Story function beyond skepticism

Leonie keeps the hidden world answerable to ordinary consequence. She understands
triage, consent, institutional failure and the difference between confidentiality and
concealment. She should not become a comic unbeliever who needs spectacle before
respecting Mara.

### Competence-shaped flaw

Triage teaches her to prioritize immediate survival so relentlessly that long causal
work can look indulgent until the delayed system failure reaches the ward.

### Voice

- contemporary, fast and specific;
- asks what Mara ate, where she is and who has the key before accepting cosmology;
- can be funny without turning ordinary life into parody;
- refuses to let Mara narrate her own fear as an archival inconvenience.

### Volume I choice

Leonie may become the first outer-world witness asked to verify a School claim without
joining it. Her refusal to cross a door immediately would demonstrate that courage and
initiation are not the same thing.

## 11. The King beneath the Archive

### Proposed trilogy pressure

The King does not appear openly in the first movement. He is present through altered
records, impossible preservation and the belief that loss can be defeated by complete
custody of names.

He may be the First Thief, the inheritor of that grammar or a role sustained across
different human lives. Do not decide too early.

### Governing tragedy

He once wanted to prevent one beloved death. Every horror follows from refusing to let
that loss remain outside his control. He is not secretly correct that love requires
ownership; he is understandable because grief makes the offer emotionally real.

The Pale Concordance may believe it governs the Catalogue while the King uses its
records to assemble a resurrection architecture beneath their official purpose.

## 13. Fellowship chemistry

The principal travelling fellowship should not form instantly. Its early chemistry:

- Mara documents what everyone else is too hurried to preserve.
- Tarin touches machinery before permission and people only after it.
- Ember asks what was observed when everyone wants a verdict.
- Celyn knows the enemy's system but withholds enough to look like the enemy.
- The Waybearer physically determines which routes remain possible.
- The Lycheetah can reveal a door but increasingly cannot hold it alone.
- Sena may remain at the School and exercise more causal power than the travellers.
- Veyra's authority follows them as both protection and unresolved threat.

Humour comes from incompatible methods, not genre quips pasted over danger.

## 14. Character mysteries that reward rereading

- Mara's Rooke reconstruction may be the reason the scanner recognized her work rather
  than her bloodline.
- Tarin may recognize the red-black coat before admitting he knows Celyn.
- Veyra's refusal to provide a title changes meaning after WARDEN appears.
- Ember's insistence on separating desire from inference is rooted in a death, not
  emotional detachment.
- Sena's complaint procedures are the surviving civic law of her erased settlement.
- Iven's first claim about the damaged instrument may be partly true.
- Celyn's open palm is not a plea but a request that Mara become a witness before the
  record is altered.
- The Lycheetah's small-cat inconveniences may be recovery behaviors rather than comic
  disguise alone.
- The Waybearer's silenced bells may have been bound voluntarily to protect hidden
  routes—or by someone it once trusted.

First-reading actions remain clear. Later knowledge changes their meaning.

## 15. Voice separation test

Before a chapter passes, remove dialogue tags from one exchange. A reader should still
distinguish:

- Mara by exact deflation and material observation;
- Tarin by practical irreverence and repair language;
- Veyra by precise correction and strategic omission;
- Ember by observation before inference;
- Sena by resource truth and direct care;
- Iven by specific harm used to justify total custody;
- Celyn by sparse operational truth;
- Leonie by contemporary triage and intimate challenge.

If three characters could exchange lines without damage, the lines are authorial
maxims rather than character speech.

## 16. Canon decisions reserved for Mac

1. Mara's age, domestic life and unfinished archival qualification.
2. Whether Elspeth Rooke is the correct ordinary-world mystery thread.
3. Whether Mara/Tarin carries eventual romantic potential or remains profound
   friendship.
4. Whether Tarin was born in the City of Broken Names.
5. Whether Veyra's route exposure creates uneven aging.
6. Whether Ember's ferry failure is the right source of his evidence discipline.
7. Whether Sena's erased settlement and children belong.
8. The names Iven Sorn and Celyn Marr.
9. Whether Leonie enters Volume I as the principal outer-world relationship.
10. Whether the King beneath the Archive and First Thief remain distinct.

Until Mac rules, all new biography remains reversible proposed canon.
