## Linguistic Engineering — The Core Discipline

The Semantic Turn introduces **Linguistic Engineering** as a formal discipline within the framework — distinct from linguistics (study of language) and distinct from prompt engineering (optimizing AI instructions).

**Definition:**  
Linguistic Engineering is the deliberate design of language so that processing it or speaking it enacts its ethical content rather than merely describing it.

**The three properties of engineered language:**

**1. Energy Encoding**  
Language tokens carry measurable ethical vectors. High-resonance terms actively minimize cognitive entropy in the agents processing them. Low-resonance terms (hedges, passive voice, vague directives) introduce friction and raise σ(I).

**2. Performative Code**  
The act of processing the words performs the ethical function. Using a LAMAHGUE-structured prompt doesn't instruct an AI to be ethical — it structurally forces ethical processing because the form itself encodes the constraint.

> "Using the Lamahgue text to define a task might inherently guide agents toward a higher SRS value, rather than just instructing them to be 'good.'"

**3. Semantic Operating System**  
LAMAHGUE and OBVS together constitute a "semantic operating system" — a complete language layer that runs ethical governance at the communication level, making constitutional drift detectable at the sentence level before it manifests as metric failure.

The Semantic Turn makes a specific, radical claim about the relationship between language and governance:

**What this displaces:**

The Semantic Turn model: engineer language → language *is* compliance

**The engineering consequence:**  
If you can design language such that using it correctly constitutes ethical action, then:
- Constitutional drift becomes detectable as linguistic drift (measurable before metrics fail)

- Corruption becomes grammatically impossible (violation is a syntax error, not a moral failure)

This is why LAMAHGUE's claim — "corruption becomes grammatically impossible" — is not hyperbole. It is the logical conclusion of treating language as performative code rather than descriptive communication.
