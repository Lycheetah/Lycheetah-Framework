## The small graph argument in §IV

Let G = (V, E) be a finite directed graph representing one declared workflow, s its
start, and T the set of acceptable completion nodes. Assume at least one path from s
to T exists. Let v differ from s and all members of T. Suppose every s-to-T path
contains v. Removing v and its incident edges leaves no s-to-T path: any surviving
path would already have been a path in G that omitted v, contradicting the premise.

This is an elementary bottleneck argument. It is not a novel graph theorem, a measure
of consciousness, or a scalar definition of human freedom. In practical use, an edge
must represent a transition that really is available under the declared permissions,
costs and accessibility conditions. A hidden dependency or a newly introduced route
can change the graph and its conclusion. Repeated routes through the same mandatory
service do not remove that service's bottleneck; a viable bypass can.

## Opposing arguments worth keeping

1. **Metaphors can hide missing mechanisms.** A memory with seasons can amount to
   ordinary versioning and cache invalidation. An explicit comparison must determine
   what, if anything, is added.
2. **Portability can cost reliability.** Some central services make dependable access
   possible. The analysis must include the price and harm of the alternative, rather
   than treat decentralisation as intrinsically good.
3. **Revision can become capture.** Stable safeguards may be more valuable than a
   frequent ability to rewrite rules. Identify which protections remain in force and
   who can authorise change.
4. **Dissent can waste time or camouflage power.** Preserve relevant objections and
   examine consequences. Neither unanimity nor disagreement is a quality score.
5. **An archive has several stakeholders.** An individual's present preference does
   not settle every retention dispute. The essay supplies no general legal rule for
   deletion, ownership or institutional records.
6. **Assistance can be an enduring good.** A person may want or need ongoing support.
   The proposal concerns usable choice and benefit, not a test of personal worth or
   an obligation to become independent of all tools.
7. **The lens may add language, not performance.** If conventional implementations
   match every useful outcome, report that. Interpretive value may survive, but a
   performance or novelty claim would not be established.
