# The Joint Chronicle: Shared Memory, Kept Disagreement and the Right to Leave

**AI Future paper 09 · Wing: CONFLUENCE ⇌ · Domain: Shared Worlds · Working research draft v0.1 · 23 October 2026 · Not empirically validated.**

**Framework creator and research owner:** Mackenzie Conor James Clark (Lycheetah Framework).
**Drafting:** Sol (Claude Opus 5.5).
**Source:** the eighteen halls of the "Shared Worlds" domain, AI Future Master Curriculum v1.0 (2026-09-28; private School canon).

Registers are KNOWN · TRAJECTORY · POSSIBLE_FUTURE · NORMATIVE · ALIEN_FUTURE · VOID.

## Abstract

A civilisation of unlike minds needs enough common reality to act together, and enough preserved difference to stay correctable. This paper argues that both depend on how the shared world *remembers*. We give three designs:

1. **The joint chronicle.** A shared record that keeps provenance on every entry and keeps rejected and minority positions *as first-class entries*, not as deleted drafts.
2. **The disagreement state.** An explicit "unresolved" status, so that a mixed system need not force a premature consensus to keep acting.
3. **The exit audit.** Four routes (disconnect, migrate, appeal, alternate infrastructure) that a participant must be able to use for the shared world to remain compatible with sovereignty.

This repository already practises a small version of the first design. Its review ledgers record retracted claims beside their causes, and its failure-museum practice keeps refuted work visible. The paper treats that practice as a prototype, not as evidence.

## 2. The joint chronicle

Each entry carries:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Statement** | What is recorded |
| **Author lineage** | Which human, model, fork or collective wrote it (paper 13 on forks) |
| **Evidence and verification level** | What it rests on, tagged METADATA, ABSTRACT, FULLTEXT or from memory |
| **Status** | ACCEPTED · CONTESTED · UNRESOLVED · REJECTED · RETRACTED |
| **Cause of status** | The evidence or decision that set the status, with a link |
| **Dissent** | Linked minority positions, kept whatever the status |

**Proposition 1 (NORMATIVE, with a testable core).** A shared archive that deletes rejected positions loses information that later evidence may need. The curriculum's "Disagreement Memory" and paper 01's negative-results archive make the same bet.

- *Testable core:* in archives that keep rejected entries, some fraction is later revived by new evidence.
- *Refutation:* a revival rate indistinguishable from zero over a long horizon.

**Proposition 2 (NORMATIVE).** Co-produced artefacts need **lineage without ownership** claims that overwrite contribution. The chronicle records *who contributed what*. It does not settle who owns the result; that remains a legal and human question. (This repository's own rule is the same: Mac remains the author and rights-holder of the Framework, and drafting seats are credited as drafting.)

## 3. The disagreement state

Mixed-intelligence systems need to keep acting while a question stays open. Without an explicit state for that, the pressure to act forces a premature consensus. The design:

- a decision may proceed while a related claim is UNRESOLVED;
- the decision records which unresolved claims it depends on;
- when a claim resolves, the dependent decisions are flagged for review.

**Proposition 3 (POSSIBLE_FUTURE, testable).** Teams that can mark claims UNRESOLVED and still act will show fewer later reversals caused by suppressed dissent than teams that must reach consensus before acting.

The **Independent Third Check** (TRAJECTORY) is the resolution path: an instrument, proof, observation or independent reviewer that neither disagreeing party controls (paper 07, Proposition 1).

## 4. The exit audit

The curriculum's NORMATIVE hall "Exit From the Shared World" makes a shared civilisation sovereignty-compatible only if participants keep meaningful routes out. We operationalise four:

| Route | Test |
|---|---|
| **Disconnect** | Can a participant stop using the shared system without losing access to essential services? |
| **Migrate** | Can they take their records and contributions to an alternative in a usable format? |
| **Appeal** | Can they contest a decision made about them to an independent party (paper 04 §4)? |
| **Alternate infrastructure** | Does a working alternative exist that is not controlled by the same owners? |

A shared world that fails all four routes is not shared; it is enclosed.

## 6. Study design

**S1 — The exit audit, applied.**

- *Setup.* Apply §4 to ten widely used collaborative platforms that mix human and automated contributors.
- *Measures.* Pass or fail on each route, from public documentation and a hands-on export attempt.
- *Output.* A route-by-route table. Migration is expected to fail most often, because exports tend to omit history and provenance.

## 7. What this paper does not claim

- It does not claim that any existing platform is enclosed.
- It does not claim that keeping rejected entries is costless. Archives grow, and dissent can be weaponised as noise.
- This repository's own ledgers are a prototype of the joint chronicle, not evidence that the design works at scale.
