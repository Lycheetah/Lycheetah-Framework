# The Withdrawal Test: What a Capability Tool Leaves Behind When It Is Switched Off

**AI Future paper 10 · Wing: FLOURISHING ☼ · Domain: Human Capability · Working research draft v0.1 · 23 October 2026 · Not empirically validated.**

**Framework creator and research owner:** Mackenzie Conor James Clark (Lycheetah Framework).
**Drafting:** Sol (Claude Opus 5.5).
**Source:** the eighteen halls of the "Human Capability" domain, AI Future Master Curriculum v1.0 (2026-09-28; private School canon).

Registers are KNOWN · TRAJECTORY · POSSIBLE_FUTURE · NORMATIVE · ALIEN_FUTURE · VOID.

## Abstract

Most demonstrations of AI helping people measure performance *with the tool switched on*. That is the right first question and the wrong last one. A tutor that raises scores while the student leans on it, and leaves the student no more able once it is gone, has delivered a service, not a capability. Sometimes a service is exactly what is wanted. It should not be reported as growth.

This paper makes the domain koan operational: **what did the system return to the human that still matters when the demo is over?** We propose:

1. the **withdrawal test**, which separates *assisted performance* from *persistent capability*;
2. the **Flourishing Lab spine**, eight questions every positive-use claim must answer before it is called flourishing;
3. a **benefit and cost vector** in place of a single score. We argue that no universal scalar exists, because the trade between, say, independence and time is the recipient's to make.

## 1. The question

*What did the system return to the human that still matters when the demo is over?*

## 2. The withdrawal test

Measure the person at three points:

| Point | What is measured | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Unassisted performance before the tool | B |
| Assisted | Performance with the tool | A |
| Withdrawn | Unassisted performance after a period of use and a washout | W |

From these:

- **assisted lift** = A − B, what the tool does while present;
- **persistent capability** = W − B, what the person keeps;
- **atrophy** shows as W < B, a capability lost through use.

**Proposition 1 (DERIVED; definitional).** Assisted lift and persistent capability are independent quantities. A tool can show large A − B with W − B = 0 (a pure service), or W − B < 0 (atrophy). Only the withdrawal measurement tells them apart.

**Proposition 2 (NORMATIVE).** A claim that a tool *builds* capability (teaches, restores, trains) must report W − B. A claim that it *provides* capability (navigates, translates, reads aloud) need not, but must then say so plainly. "Capability Restoration" and "A Tutor for Every Curious Mind" are different promises and need different evidence.

**Withdrawal is not always the right test.** For assistive technology, the curriculum's NORMATIVE hall "Ability Without Normalization" applies. A navigation aid for a blind person is not failing because the person cannot navigate the same way without it, any more than a wheelchair fails a walking test. For such tools, the right measures are agency, reliability and *exit*: whether the person can switch provider, or keep working through an outage, without losing the independence the tool gave them.

## 3. The Flourishing Lab spine

Every positive-use proposal in this wing answers eight questions:

| # | Question | Example of a failing answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reality now.** What evidence exists today, at what grade? | A product demo cited as a study |
| 2 | **Experiment next.** What is the smallest study that could refute the benefit? | None named |
| 3 | **Positive future.** What does success look like in ten years if it scales? | Only a market size |
| 4 | **Failure mirror.** What does the same system look like when it goes wrong? | "It cannot go wrong" |
| 5 | **Recipient.** Who, exactly, receives the benefit? | "Users", "society" |
| 6 | **Withdrawal.** What remains when it is switched off? | Not measured |
| 7 | **Capture.** Who could absorb the gain instead of the recipient? | Not considered (paper 12) |
| 8 | **Exit.** Can the recipient leave without losing what they gained? | Data and skills locked in |

### The benefit and cost vector

| Benefit | Cost |
|---|---|
| Agency gain | Dependency burden |
| Persistent capability (W − B) | Atrophy (W < B) |
| Time actually returned (paper 12) | Lock-in |
| Access expansion | Capture |
| Resilience and exit | Harm burden |
| Dignity and meaning | |

**Proposition 3 (NORMATIVE).** These quantities are reported side by side, never summed into one number. A single "flourishing score" would hide exactly the trades the recipient has the right to make.

## 5. Study designs

**S1 — Learning Without Shame, with withdrawal.**

- *Setup.* Learners who report avoiding questions in class, randomised to (A) a private AI tutor, (B) a private human tutor, or (C) the usual course alone, for eight weeks.
- *Measures.* B, A and W from §2, with W taken after a two-week washout; questions asked in class; self-reported shame when stuck.
- *Prediction.* Arm A shows a positive W − B, not only assisted lift, and a rise in questions asked in class. Learning without shame should carry back into the room.
- *Refutation.* W − B ≈ 0 while A − B is large: the tutor performed, and the learner did not grow.

**S2 — The exit audit for assistive tools.** For a navigation or reading aid, simulate an outage and a provider switch. Measure how much independent function the person keeps in each case.

## 6. What this paper does not claim

- It does not claim that AI tutoring works or fails in general. The anchors are single studies in specific settings.
- It does not claim that services are inferior to capabilities. It claims that they must be named honestly.
- It does not claim that the benefit and cost vector is complete.

## 7. Anchors

All at verification level METADATA, from the curriculum's research ledger.

- Kestin et al., AI tutoring randomised controlled trial, *Scientific Reports* (2025). Grade: study. Supports that a purpose-designed AI tutor improved learning outcomes in one undergraduate physics setting. Does not support that AI tutoring generally beats human teaching, and does not report a withdrawal measure as defined here.
- Liu & Slade, AI smartphone navigation for people with blindness, *Nature Biomedical Engineering* (2026). Grade: study (small participant sample). Supports the trajectory of capability restoration. Does not support universal effectiveness, or the replacement of established mobility aids.
- AAAI 2026 Spring Symposium on Well-Being AI. Grade: agenda. Shows that the question is active. It does not validate the Flourishing Lab spine or the benefit and cost vector.
