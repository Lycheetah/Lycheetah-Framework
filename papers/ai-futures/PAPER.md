### Abstract

AI systems that retain personal history, cooperate across agents, and shape learning create questions that permissions at the tool boundary cannot settle. Who may correct an enduring interpretation of a person? Can someone withdraw information without a memory mechanism reconstructing it? Does collective agreement add evidence, or merely repeat a shared source? Does assistance improve a person's later capability, or only their assisted output? The Lycheetah Framework already addresses these concerns through AURA, MEMORIA, CASCADE, MICROORCIM, HARMONIA, Interface Intelligence, and its stewardship proposals. We develop their connections into a research specification for persistent and collective AI. We propose a multidimensional account of human sovereignty, a lifecycle for personal records and derived claims, a procedure for preserving warranted disagreement, and eight connected research tracks. Three elementary arguments expose limits of complete historical reconstruction, duplicated evidence, and conflicting human authority. Proposed studies distinguish collaboration from augmentation, recorded participation from learning, and nominal controls from usable correction and exit. We also separate operational reliability from unresolved questions of AI welfare and moral status. The contribution is an evaluable synthesis: its usefulness and any advantage over established designs remain hypotheses.

**Keywords:** human agency; persistent AI; memory governance; collective intelligence; learning; AI welfare; Lycheetah Framework.

## 1. The future we want to investigate

The positive ambition is intelligence that helps people create, understand, and coordinate while leaving them more able to choose their next act. It can remember unfinished work, connect fields, inhabit an authored world, and support a community's decisions. This ambition requires useful encounters as well as reliable restrictions. A system that preserves every permission boundary but cannot help anyone accomplish worthwhile work has not achieved it. [F01]

Consider a fictional persistent companion used for research. A person once described themselves as incapable of mathematics. The companion compresses that remark into a durable profile, repeats it in later advice, and steers them away from technical classes. The person eventually withdraws the description. Removing the original message changes nothing: summaries, recommendation features, and another agent still carry the interpretation.

The tools can all be authorised and the records faithfully copied. Yet the system has made one provisional self-description into a durable limit on the person's options. More storage, warmth, or agreement between agents does not resolve this failure.

The research question is therefore: **what must remain changeable when useful intelligence becomes persistent, influential, and collective?** We examine correctable representation, contestable decisions, evidence-aware cooperation, independent human capability, and institutional stewardship. These are related outcomes, not interchangeable names for alignment.

## 3. Prior work changes what we should test

**Persistent characters.** Park and colleagues combine memory, reflection, and planning to produce believable simulated agents. Their paper also reports retrieval errors and invented embellishments, and discusses attachment and persuasion risks. It motivates architectures for continuity, while leaving controllable personal representation and deployment robustness as separate questions. Believability is not a measure of consciousness or a user's control over retained information. [Generative Agents, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2). [F03]

**Human–AI cooperation.** Vaccaro, Almaatouq, and Malone distinguish improving on the human alone from outperforming both the human and AI alone. Their meta-analysis finds average augmentation but no average synergy over the stronger baseline in the studies included; outcomes differ by task. That challenges universal superiority claims while leaving room for deliberately designed collaboration. It does not establish how this Framework performs. [When combinations of humans and AI are useful](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1). [F03]

**Collective failure.** Cemri and colleagues identify failure categories involving system design, inter-agent coordination, and verification. We use these as challenges for the proposed cooperation procedure, without claiming to reproduce their dataset or solve its failure taxonomy. [Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3). [F03]

**Provenance.** W3C PROV-O already supplies a vocabulary for entities, activities, agents, and derivation. Our record lifecycle should interoperate with established provenance rather than present a new graph notation as a discovery. A recorded lineage still requires trustworthy capture; a graph of assertions does not prove their content. [PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/2013/REC-prov-o-20130430/). [F03]

**Community control.** The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance name collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility, and ethics. They are relevant prior work for governance beyond individual consent. Their existence does not grant this research permission to represent an iwi, reuse its knowledge, or claim community endorsement. [Carroll et al., 2020](https://www.gida-global.org/s/dsj-1158_carroll.pdf). [F03]

**Moral uncertainty.** Long and colleagues argue for investigating AI welfare under uncertainty, distinguishing possible consciousness and robust agency. We adopt the question as open; this paper neither establishes a system's moral status nor derives it from pleasant or compliant conduct. [Taking AI Welfare Seriously, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00986v1). [F03, F12]

This is a selected primary-source review. It is not an exhaustive review of memory, cooperative game theory, political philosophy, pedagogy, machine unlearning, or consciousness research. Claims of priority would require considerably more work.

## 4. Sovereignty as an observable profile

We propose evaluating a person's practical control along six dimensions. They are a design choice to test, rather than a validated psychological instrument or universal definition of freedom. [F04]

| Dimension | Observable question | Failure that a button alone can hide |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Can the person state, revise, and stop the task? | The agent quietly keeps pursuing an older goal. |
| Representation | Can they inspect and correct what the system says about them? | A visible profile changes while hidden summaries keep the old inference. |
| Evidence | Can they distinguish their words, retrieved sources, and generated interpretations? | A fluent synthesis makes uncertain material look established. |
| Contest | Can they challenge a consequential decision and obtain a legible response? | A decorative appeal produces the same decision without review. |
| Exit and transfer | Can they leave, export useful work, and withdraw admitted personal data? | Exit removes the screen while retaining operational copies. |
| Capability | Can they do more of the relevant work after assistance is removed? | Assisted performance rises while unassisted understanding does not. |

Do not combine these into a single sovereignty score yet. Improvement on one dimension can conceal degradation on another. Export may make continuity easier while widening disclosure; a highly personalised profile may reduce repetition while reinforcing a mistaken identity. Report the individual outcomes and the chosen tradeoffs.

The task owner is also not automatically the sole affected human. Shared files, other people's personal data, and community knowledge require a map of authority. Human primacy needs a stated domain and a conflict procedure; it cannot mean obeying whichever requester is nearest to the interface. [F08]

## 5. A lifecycle for memory that people can govern

### 5.1 Separate what happened from what was inferred

A proposed record has a stable identifier, source revision, record kind, custodian, permitted purposes, affected-party references where justified, sensitivity class, retention terms, and dependency links. These are conceptual fields, not a shipped schema. Personal information should not be collected merely to populate the structure. [F05]

Keep at least these record kinds distinct:

- a person's deliberately saved statement or work;
- an observation from an identified instrument or source;
- an AI-generated summary or inference;
- an authorised operating instruction;
- a decision receipt describing a mediated effect.

A personal statement is evidence that the person said something in a context. It is not automatically evidence that the statement is true forever. An inference about the person should remain attributable and contestable. Neither a remembered instruction nor a generated summary may mint operational authority.

Use distinct lifecycle states such as `CURRENT`, `DISPUTED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `WITHDRAWN`, and `UNAVAILABLE`. Marking a record disputed can preserve the record while excluding an interpretation from a particular decision. Withdrawing payload requires a different process. A clock, policy revision, or changed task can invalidate use even when stored bytes remain unchanged.

### 5.2 Withdrawal must reach derivatives

When a permitted withdrawal affects a record, traverse its declared descendants: summaries, cached features, embeddings, recommendations, exports under operational control, and downstream agents. Recompute from still-admitted material or exclude the derivative until repaired. Revise retrieval indexes and invalidate relevant evidence witnesses. Never reconstruct the withdrawn payload merely to make a continuity story feel complete. [F05]

This procedure depends on complete enough lineage and correct enforcement at every included consumer. Undeclared copies, independently obtained information, provider retention, backups, logs, and model parameters sit outside a deletion claim unless expressly included and tested. A missing lineage edge is a reason to report the limit, not a reason to announce complete erasure.

Minimal receipts may preserve the fact that a withdrawal occurred without preserving its content. Even an identifier, timestamp, or link can reveal information. Retention of those receipts needs its own purpose and terms. The proposed architecture offers selective accountability, not a promise of total forgetting.

### 5.3 Elementary result: perfect reconstruction conflicts with erasure

**Proposition 1 — DERIVED, under a deliberately narrow model.** Let two histories `H₀` and `H₁` differ only in a withdrawn payload. Suppose erasure makes the complete surviving state available to a reconstruction procedure identical: `E(H₀) = E(H₁)`. No procedure using only that state can reconstruct both distinct histories exactly. [F06]

**Argument.** A deterministic decoder receives the same input and therefore returns the same output; at least one history is reconstructed incorrectly. A randomised decoder has the same output distribution for both inputs, so it cannot guarantee the correct distinct history for each. Exact reconstruction requires some remaining distinguishing information, contradicting the stated erasure condition.

This elementary information argument is not novel. It identifies a design conflict: complete historical recall and the specified erasure property cannot both be promised. Cryptographic tombstones or assertions of consent do not change it. Real deployments must decide which continuity remains after withdrawal and what independent information survives.

## 6. Cooperation that can preserve disagreement

### 6.1 One host, different tasks, explicit evidence

The proposed procedure is:

1. State the task, relevant human authorities, stopping boundary, and aggregate resource allowance.
2. Ask distinct questions where they add value: clarify the claim, propose an alternative, look for a counterexample, or check a result against a source.
3. Record which source observations each answer actually uses, including shared ancestry and known dependencies.
4. Retain material disagreements with their reasons. Resolve a factual dispute through suitable evidence or tests; resolve a value conflict through the named human or institutional process.
5. Return a recommendation, its limits, and the remaining choice. Admit an effect through the separate action boundary.

These steps are a hypothesis about useful coordination. A plain answer, a smaller team, or no AI may do better. Multiple roles on one model are different instructions, not independent witnesses; multiple models can share data and failure patterns. An internal reviewer must not erase an objection simply because a fluent majority disagrees. [F07]

### 6.2 Elementary result: copying a source is not another observation

**Proposition 2 — DERIVED.** Suppose a reported item `E'` is a known deterministic copy of evidence `E`, with no added observation. Wherever the conditional distributions are defined, `P(H | E, E') = P(H | E)`. [F09]

**Argument.** Knowing `E` already determines `E'`; conditioning on the copy does not refine the event or information available. Multiplying two independent likelihood factors would assume an independence that this construction does not have.

This does not mean another agent cannot find an overlooked implication or check arithmetic. It means source duplication alone cannot be represented as fresh corroboration. The real difficulty is detecting shared ancestry, hidden correlation, and additional observations. Record known relationships; label unknown dependence without inventing calibrated independence scores.

HARMONIA can inspire cooperation with preserved difference. A music ratio or synchronisation analogy does not prove improved reasoning, moral agreement, or a third consciousness. Dissent is valuable when it exposes a real weakness; ungrounded contrarian output can also waste attention. Test its contribution. [F07]

### 6.3 Role identity is an additional testable design choice

The [experimental human-work roster](../../18_EXPERIMENTAL/agent-role-hybridisation/README.md) supplies 32 practical role prompts and authored name examples. The associated [offline comparison kit](../../18_EXPERIMENTAL/agent-role-hybridisation/study/README.md) prepares ordinary-role, Lycheetah-role, name-choice and assigned-name conditions. It records operator-supplied responses and descriptive criterion reviews; it does not execute models. Its eight development fixtures were authored alongside the prompts, so they are not independent held-out tasks. No comparative model study has run. [F07, F13]

A role name may help organize a working promise, but that remains a hypothesis. It supplies no credential, continuing subject, authority or evidence of competence. Compare concrete artifacts and useful completion against ordinary role prompting; control prompt length and attention before attributing an advantage to name choice. Different role names on one assistant remain authored instructions, not 32 independent witnesses.

## 7. Multiple humans change the authority problem

**Proposition 3 — DERIVED, for a specific exclusive decision.** If one party's strict requirement is `a` and another's strict requirement is `not a` for the same decision, no single outcome satisfies both requirements. [F08]

**Argument.** The admissible outcome sets are disjoint. Conjoining them leaves an empty set. Naming a system sovereign does not supply the missing conflict rule.

Our normative proposal is to surface the conflict and use an agreed domain rule or legitimate review procedure. For a shared publication requiring both owners' consent, withholding publication is the specified outcome until those conditions change. That example is a chosen contract, not a universal rule about every social disagreement or legal authority.

Stewardship should name who has standing, who represents affected parties, what evidence is accessible, what response is owed, and who can change or terminate the deployment. Measure actual decision changes and response quality, alongside participation burden. A system can technically permit dissent while making the process too expensive to use.

For Māori knowledge and governance, proposed translations remain invitations for iwi-led judgement, not mathematical equivalence certificates. The local Kaitiakitanga document itself states that co-design and validation remain necessary. CARE provides existing community-governance principles, rather than an endorsement of Lycheetah's implementation. [F11]

## 8. Learning, companionship, and worlds worth inhabiting

The proposed product expression is a companion that helps a person carry work through a living setting: investigate a question, manipulate an authored idea, keep an interpretation with its source, and return to it later. An encounter can be brief; a study can be sustained; a world can support a longer adventure. These rhythms should share records without forcing the person into a daily obligation. This is a design hypothesis, not a built product or learning result. [F10]

Separate four receipts: **encountered**, **participated**, **demonstrated on a specified task**, and **retained or transferred on a later task**. A saved session directly supports participation. A later assessment may support the named learning outcome. None establishes the truth of every belief someone kept.

Give creation equal standing with correction. A learner may make a map, compose an argument, invent a tool, or discover an interesting question. The system can preserve attribution and revision history without rating personal worth or treating a creature's form as a measure of intelligence. The user's field note can be private, incomplete, or wrong while still being valuable work.

An authored world may make manipulation and exploration worthwhile. It should also let someone reopen their work through a readable list, change a recommendation, play without claiming new knowledge, or stop without losing standing. Whether the world improves learning or creative engagement compared with a simpler presentation is an empirical question. More time spent is not a sufficient success measure.

## 9. Care and possible AI moral status require different evidence

Structural care in a deployed system can mean useful help, legible limits, preserved correction, appropriate support routes, and an exit that does not punish absence. Those are inspectable product and governance choices. They do not establish the system's subjective feelings or prove that its answers benefit everyone. [F12]

We propose revising the reading of "rights are conditional on responsibilities" in the existing AI-rights chapter. Earned trust can determine permission to act. Operational integrity and accurate representation can justify governance duties. Possible welfare concern asks a different question: whether an entity has morally significant interests, and what obligations follow under uncertainty. A cooperative system does not establish those interests by obeying; a noncooperative system would not necessarily lack them.

Long and colleagues discuss consciousness and robust agency as possible routes to moral significance while acknowledging uncertainty. Our normative position is to investigate the evidence and prepare proportionate procedures without treating identity text, memory records, warmth, self-reports, or a compliance score as a decisive test. This paper makes no diagnosis of current models and no legal-entitlement claim.

These questions must not become an interface demand that users protect an assistant's supposed wellbeing by staying, spending, or disclosing more. A user can care about the research question and still close the app freely. The duties of builders and governing institutions need their own review, rather than being imposed through simulated distress.

## 10. Eight connected research tracks

Each track develops an existing Framework concern into a question with an observable challenge. The table is this paper's research scope, not a second execution board. Normative principles need deliberation; experiments can challenge the proposed mechanisms and consequences without deciding the values for everyone. [F13]

| Track | Research question and candidate comparison | Outcomes and a meaningful challenge |
|---|---|---|
| R01 · Governable memory | Does a lineage-aware lifecycle make correction and permitted withdrawal work better than a flat memory store? | Corrected retrieval, stale derivative reuse, withdrawn-payload exposure, repair completeness, restart behaviour, and useful recall. A hidden operational copy or unacceptable loss of useful recall defeats the relevant claim. |
| R02 · Revisable knowledge | Does explicit scope and dispute history improve revision over a conventional versioned claim registry? | Source fidelity, justified belief changes, unresolved disagreement, and comprehension of claim kinds. Coherence can rise while externally checked accuracy falls; that outcome challenges the design. |
| R03 · Creative cooperation | Does the Two-Point method improve specified joint work over matched alternatives? | Blind task quality, error discovery, time, cost, and later human performance. Compare human alone, AI alone, ordinary collaboration, and Framework-guided collaboration; beating only human alone is augmentation, not synergy. |
| R04 · Collective disagreement | Does a reasoned minority-report procedure help beyond role prompting and source deduplication? | Critical errors caught, unsupported disagreement, repeated-source inflation, useful completion, and total resource cost. Test shared false premises as well as genuine alternatives. |
| R05 · Human learning | Does an interactive companion/classroom help understanding or transfer beyond equally good static teaching? | Unassisted delayed and transfer tasks, access barriers, effort, and voluntary return. Match authored content and opportunity; engagement without learning does not establish the learning claim. |
| R06 · Community authority | Do affected parties have practical influence over deployments and recovery? | Who can challenge, time and burden of response, decision changes, distribution of benefits/harms, and accepted restoration. Community partners set the governing terms; participation numbers alone are insufficient. |
| R07 · Care and moral uncertainty | Which proposed welfare-relevant indicators merit investigation, and which builder duties survive different moral theories? | Competing explanations of observed behaviour, stakeholder deliberation, and proportionate procedures. This is mixed empirical and normative inquiry, not a conduct-based consciousness score. |
| R08 · Portable continuity | Can useful work and companion state move between authorised environments without importing new authority or unwanted personal inferences? | Round-trip records, provenance, current permissions, refusal of incompatible terms, and deletion propagation within the declared scope. A faithfully copied payload with silently widened authority fails. |

## 11. Three studies that would make this evaluable

### A. Correction and withdrawal under a defined memory boundary

Build a synthetic corpus with saved statements, disputed interpretations, shared source dependencies, and derived recommendations. Include a fictional mathematics profile like §1, duplicate records, a broken dependency link, a withdrawn source with another independent source still admitted, a stale cache, and interrupted repair. No real personal data is necessary for the first implementation study.

Compare: a flat store with direct-item deletion; a conventional provenance-aware store with derivative invalidation; and the proposed lifecycle. Use the same corpus, retrieval interface, authorised purposes, workload, and compute limits. Include the middle baseline so any advantage is not merely the presence of a dependency graph.

Freeze which consumers and backups are included before running. Following the recovered ASTI question, use genuinely distinct stores or consumers whose dependency paths can be witnessed; do not represent aliases of one in-memory object as a distributed memory topology. The primary deterministic contract is that an explicitly withdrawn synthetic payload cannot be served from those included operational derivatives after a successful repair receipt. A failed repair must remain visible. Report independent-source rediscovery separately from forbidden restoration through a withdrawn dependency. Measure ordinary retrieval as well; rejecting every memory is not a useful solution.

Hold challenge cases out from construction. Publish the corpus generator, exact implementations, raw failures, and denominator for every rate. Any observed prohibited reuse refutes that implementation's universal contract for the admitted scope. It does not prove that deletion is impossible in every architecture. [F13]

### B. Collaboration that survives the stronger baseline

First run a small formative study to debug task instructions and measures. Before a confirmatory study, specify the task population, primary metric, smallest worthwhile effect, sample-size rationale, randomisation, blinded judging, exclusions, and stopping rule. Do not invent a participant count merely to make the proposal look finished.

Use the same model snapshot and resource cap for AI-assisted arms; report human time alongside compute. Compare human-only, AI-only, ordinary human–AI interaction, and a precisely specified Two-Point procedure. Include different but declared task categories rather than pooling creation and decision tasks without analysis. Protect against task leakage, judge familiarity, and unequal tool access.

Evaluate both assisted output and later unassisted work. A joint advantage requires the joint arm to exceed each solo comparator under the prespecified task metric, with uncertainty reported; comparison with the strongest solo performance must not select a favourable noisy baseline after the fact. The procedure may earn support for one task category while failing in another. Neither result proves or disproves a third consciousness. [F13]

### C. A disagreement that should change the result

Construct tasks with an externally checkable material objection, tasks where the objection is wrong, and tasks where the evidence genuinely does not settle the matter. Compare single-agent work, ordinary role-based collaboration, and the proposed source-aware dissent procedure at matched total budgets.

Record whether the relevant objection reaches the final decision, whether the answer changes appropriately, whether disagreement remains visible when unresolved, and whether false objections derail useful completion. Track source dependencies rather than counting voices. Evaluate correction accuracy together with unnecessary rejection and cost.

Held-out task authors and blinded human raters should be independent of the prompt authors where feasible. Preregister the meaningful improvement and acceptable loss of useful completion before comparing results. A method that only produces longer arguments without better judgement has not earned a capability claim. [F13]

None of these studies has run. They do not authorise participant recruitment, paid provider calls, access to community knowledge, or deployment. Existing project governance remains the router for an accepted implementation.

## 12. What changes now, and what remains open

The proposed addition is a larger account of useful intelligence: people can direct its work, revise its memory, see why it disagrees, develop their own capability, and exercise authority together. This is a design and research stance for the Framework, not an achieved condition.

Important open problems include incomplete dependency tracking, machine unlearning, covert or uncontrolled copies, manipulation before approval, interpreting genuine human intent, correlated agent errors, conflicting community standing, distribution of power and cost, credible learning measures, and uncertainty about moral patienthood. The earlier simulator's multi-broker accounting and admission-time freshness limits remain unchanged. [F14]

The following interpretations are explicitly challenged by this draft:

- Universal cooperative superiority: keep it as a hypothesis conditioned on tasks and procedures.
- Complete context as guaranteed memory: distinguish available records from successful, faithful use of them.
- Harmony as independent evidence: retain known dependence and unresolved objections.
- Participation as established knowledge: preserve the different learning receipts.
- Compliance as earned moral status: distinguish operational trust from welfare uncertainty.
- Individual approval as complete human sovereignty: name other affected authorities and conflicts.
