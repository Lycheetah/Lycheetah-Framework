# Failure Museum — Forge IV

## Confirmed local behaviours
- The toy engine computes frontier and applies assumed authorization/deadline gating (14 passing tests).
- On a deliberately authored 10-scenario dataset, eight examples expose more than one non-dominated choice.
- No external action is taken and a human must select an option after the tool's presentation.

## Demonstrated failure classes
- Fake option families inflate counted independent outcome space (`counterexample_results.json`: 1 true vs 20 claimed).
- False independent-execution metadata produces a positive count for an actually vendor-gated outcome (0 vs 1).
- Thirty unique options are counted despite no modeling of attention/review burden.
- No stochastic feasibility, calibrated probability, cross-context values or accessibility constraints.
- `authorized=True` is a toy input; real authentication and action grants belong to Forge II–III.
- A self-reported `verified=True` is not a signed provider receipt or physical demonstration.

## Non-claims
- No proof of improved human sovereignty, free will, consciousness, entropy relation, or structural welfare metric.
- No A/B study, no real human trial, no secure proof of provider exit, no deployment or production integration.
- No claim that larger frontier is always better; valuable commitments can intentionally reduce options.
- No claim of algorithmic originality of Pareto fronts, capability sets, or empowerment research.

## Required next challenges
- Honest independent outcome labels, real provider migration/export checks, actual user choice comprehension, probability calibration, and evaluation under concrete opportunity cost.
- Strongest baseline: ordinary portable file formats plus clearly presented consent and undo, not a pure throughput maximizer alone.
