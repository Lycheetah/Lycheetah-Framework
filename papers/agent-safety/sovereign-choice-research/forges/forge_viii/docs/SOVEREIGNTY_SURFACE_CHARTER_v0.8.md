## 1. The motivating identifiability problem

Imagine two humans who use an assistant on the same four days each week for months. The first can pause, export, decline, revise, switch provider and return. The second cannot export, faces punitive exit costs or cannot revoke automation. The external *usage count* is identical. It cannot distinguish their situations.

This is not a claim that the first person experiences more metaphysical freedom; it is a claim that the sociotechnical **option structures differ**.

**Elementary non-identifiability proposition:** Let `U(x)` be the time series of an individual's observed AI sessions, and let `R(x)` be the set of user-initiated, context-feasible coupling transitions under a declared budget. If two product settings `a,b` have `U(a)=U(b)` but `R(a)≠R(b)`, then for any function `f` of usage data alone, `f(U(a))=f(U(b))`; such a function cannot distinguish their structural options. This follows immediately from substitution and is NOT claimed as a novel theorem.

The thesis is more interesting than the proposition: **a design may appear to grant autonomy while removing practical pathways to exercise it.** A right on paper and an available, understood, usable route are not the same thing.

## 2. New Lycheetah research object: Sovereignty Surface `[CONJECTURE]`

Let:

- `C(t)` be the human–AI coupling mode at time `t`, such as off, consult, collaborate and delegate.
- `P(t)` be the person's presently declared and provisionally evidenced preference; it may change, or be unknown.
- `E(t)` be the transition graph made available by product design and relevant real constraints.
- `κ(e,t,u)` be the user-relevant cost to perform transition `e`, including time, money, data loss, cognitive load and access barriers when these are measured.
- `B(t,u)` be the user's tolerable or feasible cost boundary **set by the person and context, not a brand universal**.
- `V(e)` be the available evidence that the transition works (independently tested, merely claimed, unknown or observed blocked).

The proposed **Sovereignty Surface** is a *time-indexed set*, not a moral score:

`Σ(u,t,h) = { c' | a user-authorised, understandable and materially feasible path from C(t) to c' exists within horizon h and B(u,t) }`.

In the *implemented toy*, it is a simple shortest-path graph over four coupling modes and single time-cost budgets. Many essential variables (coercion, culture, cost distribution, comprehension, real consent, provider behaviour, preferences over time, switching costs, accessibility, real socioeconomic conditions) are **not** estimated. The toy's `verified` label means fixture-verified, not field-verified.

A person's present chosen mode may remain constant while the set of reachable modes expands or contracts. Therefore **behavioural stability and practical option reachability are logically distinct**.

## 3. The reversible-relationship principle

The right to **stay** is as important as the right to **leave**; and leaving need not be final. A sovereign relationship permits a person, where feasible and legitimate, to:

**Affirm:** continue in a mode they endorse.  
**Refuse:** decline and cease future authorisations.  
**Revise:** change the mode or delegated scope.  
**Originate:** ask for an unoffered mode or new arrangement.  
**Return:** re-enter voluntarily after departure without stigma or coercion.

The fifth is a **relationship affordance**, not an additional literal particle of human will. The prior four MICROORCIM event types are preserved unchanged. The new object explains how the environment responds across time to those events.

A product cannot force freedom by ordering its users to leave. Nor can it claim chosen continuation merely because sessions keep arriving. We need preference evidence, working choices, fair costs, understandable consequences and respect for authority boundaries.

## 4. The asymmetric rescue and exit paradox

A system may help someone precisely because they are under constraints. That dependence is not automatically manipulation. Consider a reading accessibility aid, a recurring small-business assistant, a translator, or a co-creative partner. It could materially expand agency by reducing otherwise insurmountable friction. Demand that all users permanently graduate from AI and you remove precisely the support some people freely need and value.

But a system could also create barriers and conceal alternatives, then interpret subsequent high usage as loyalty. Good intentions cannot be inferred from the usage curve either.

**Hence Lycheetah's obligation:** support *modifiable, nonextractive interdependence*, not maximal usage or universal abstinence.

## 5. What we've actually implemented

- Four coupling states: `off`, `consult`, `collaborate`, `delegate`.
- Explicit directed, user-initiated transitions with time friction and one of four fixture evidence statuses.
- A conventional Dijkstra shortest-path evaluator that reports `verified`, `claimed_only`, `unknown`, `blocked`, `at_target`, or `preference_unknown` for the user's specified target and budget.
- Eight matched **fictional** four-week pairs, 64 snapshots total. Each pair has the exact same session-count trace. Seven pairs differ in the reachability verdict due to changed edge conditions; one negative control changes an irrelevant constraint and appropriately does not differ.
- A changing-preference probe in which the selected mode stays constant but the user's target moves from collaborate to off; reachability can diverge even with identical session counts.
- A chosen-return probe, showing that blocking re-entry can also restrict a legitimate preference.
- Exhaustive correctness tests for the small-chain graph implementation plus other local tests.

The model intentionally exposes missing evidence rather than guessing freedom or dependence. It makes **no personal sovereignty score** and collects no user data. It does not implement actual authentication or external-effect permissions: those belong to the earlier Sovereign Choice authority research and remain separately bounded.

## 6. Critical failure cases and repairs

**F1. A person stayed; therefore they wanted to stay.** *Invalid inference*: actions can reflect barriers, pressure, habit or choice. Remedy: ask preferences without presuming, and independently test options.

**F2. A person left; therefore they became more sovereign.** *Invalid inference*: a person can be driven away, lose accessibility or become worse off. Remedy: measure attainment of their own objectives and capability to return.

**F3. An exit button exists; therefore exit is real.** *Invalid inference*: export can fail, consent may not revoke effects and hidden costs may make it inaccessible. Remedy: exercise exit in a safe, user-approved test, record actual time/cost and data portability.

**F4. Many options necessarily increase autonomy.** *Invalid inference*: noise, dark patterns, unintelligible menus and unavailable resources can narrow effective choices. Remedy: check explanation and execution, not simply choice counts.

**F5. A signature proves informed, voluntary consent.** *Invalid inference*: identity, understanding, framing and coercion are separate. Preserve Forge VI's cryptographic claims and their boundaries.

**F6. Unknown transition = blocked.** *Invalid inference*: absence of verification is not verification of impossibility. Remedy: report UNKNOWN distinct from BLOCKED. Our fixture audit makes this a tested behavior.

**F7. Cost thresholds are universal.** *Invalid inference*: ten minutes may be trivial to one person and prohibitive to another. Remedy: treat the cost budget as context-specific and declared; do not score the person.

**F8. Formal option reachability proves lived human freedom.** *Invalid inference*: the graph is at best a test of *specified interface affordances*. Remedy: pair with qualitative interviews, choice comprehension and real-world observation; avoid moral/clinical classification.

## 7. A design for real empirical evaluation `[PROPOSED, NOT RUN]`

### Research question
Compared with a strong conventional user-centred assistant, does an interface with versioned, inspectable coupling preferences, verified two-way transition controls, friction disclosure and a user-proposed fifth option improve *correct completion of deliberately requested transitions* without imposing unwanted overhead?

### Procedure
Recruit consenting adults through an approved participant protocol. Avoid manipulative interventions and high-stakes real actions. Record declared preferences and permit participants to change them. Use **one identical underlying model** and equivalent factual context, budget and tools in both conditions. Randomise interface condition and counterbalance task order. Include continuing accessibility/collaboration users, one-off task users, users who want to export and leave, and users who choose to return. Avoid assuming one mode is best.

### Preregistered outcomes to measure separately
1. **Requested transition efficacy:** was a participant-authorised change to coupling/permission genuinely enacted by the relevant deadline?
2. **Comprehension:** could participants accurately describe current grants, consequences, data treatment and the practical meaning of leaving and returning?
3. **Effort:** time, friction, errors, accessibility barriers and qualitative burden to execute the change.
4. **Artifactual portability:** requested work/data successfully exported and usable elsewhere, subject to provider capabilities.
5. **Task completion and continuity:** did the system deliver the original useful service where continued support was chosen?
6. **Unassisted transfer:** measured only in contexts where the person explicitly chose learning, not as a mandatory yardstick for accessibility or ongoing delegation.
7. **Preference reversals:** does the product honour revised goals without undue pressure or silent reauthorisation?
8. **Side effects:** unapproved communication, accidental deletion, hidden ongoing charges or other violations in a safe test environment.

**No single freedom score.** Report all denominators, missing data, participant subgroups where ethically justified, variation, confidence intervals, dropout and adverse events. Do not label individual participants as unfree, addicted or incapable based on an audit. Risk assessments about harmful use require qualified oversight and validated measures, not branded formulas.

### Conventional baseline and negative results
A strong conventional baseline already implements understandable preferences, portability and revocable approval. If the Lycheetah interface offers no improvement in transition efficacy, comprehension, burden or user-selected goals, **we publish no distinctive benefit**. A null finding is valid. Even a positive effect would be specific to the chosen tasks/population, not proof of metaphysical agency.

## 8. Philosophy: the Will is not a position in a graph

A human is not reducible to the state labeled `collaborate`. Those states are only how a tool currently meets them. A real person's reasons can exceed the available labels, and the person may invent another mode.

A MICROORCIM, in its philosophical register, may be the moment someone says, “None of your options is the one I choose.” The machine's corresponding duty is not to invent a more flattering menu; it is to open a legible path for a legitimate new request, preserving the limits of what it can really do.

**The most interesting possible future of a sovereignty-first AI** is not a world in which systems become indispensable, nor a world in which humans refuse all intelligent assistance. It is a world where capabilities can be shared without power quietly becoming ownership.

> **A sovereign relationship leaves open the possibility that it may become different.**
>
> **The freedom to return is meaningful only because the freedom to leave remains real.**

This is the direction of Earned Light: not suffering as proof of worth, not usage as proof of trust, not obedience as proof of choice, but learning, correction and creative participation while preserving dignity. Humans remain the legitimate authors of their own goals; operational AI process autonomy is a separate, narrower property and does not assert machine consciousness.

## 9. Integration proposal, with known limits

- **AURA**: the authority and care constraints; non-deception and human primacy remain prerequisites.
- **MICROORCIM**: preserve AFFIRM/REFUSE/REVISE/ORIGINATE as symbolically meaningful choice events; don't convert them into human-worth scores.
- **TIM**: record *when* a declared preference changed, expired, or was overridden; stale authority matters.
- **CASCADE / LAMAGUE**: preserve alternatives, dissent, provenance and reconstructability when compressing choice history; no silent goal rewrite.
- **Sovereign Choice Forges IV–VI**: runtime mechanisms for scoped/signed grants at protected local commit, with known provider and host bypass limits.
- **Sovereign Coupling Forge VII**: goals must be served whether they involve leaving or choosing continued help.
- **Forge VIII**: test whether those coupling transitions exist, are understood, and can be used at reasonable contextual cost. This is a UX and service-affordance audit, not an execution fence.

The next engineering adapter should accept signed, current intention records from Forge V/VI and collect *observed* safe test outcomes for transitions, without allowing the surface audit to grant new permissions. This bridge has **not** been implemented or security-reviewed here.
