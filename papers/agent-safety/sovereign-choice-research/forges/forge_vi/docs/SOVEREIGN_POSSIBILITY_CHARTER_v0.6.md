## 1. The deeper distinction

Imagine two systems. Both obtain a valid signature for a real email. Both obey the signed payload. The first presents an accurate summary, an honest draft-only alternative, a prominent decline path, and a visible point beyond which cancellation cannot be guaranteed. The second pressures a tired person with urgency and false scarcity, hides the refusal option, and later displays the valid signature as proof of informed consent. **Cryptography cannot distinguish them.** Their execution integrity can match while their contributions to autonomy differ dramatically.

Thus **formal authorisation is necessary for certain trustworthy actions but insufficient for a full account of meaningful choice.**

Free will remains a contested metaphysical topic. We adopt a narrower engineering and product goal: maintain *counterfactual agency*, meaning that a person's legitimate decisions would actually cause different authorised outcomes — including refusal, revision and abstention — while they retain fair access to those paths.

## 2. Counterfactual Agency Horizon (CAH) — new proposed concept

**Intuition:** At a particular moment in a human–AI interaction, which alternative directions remain *meaningfully reachable* by the person, and for how long?

Let:

- `u`: person or properly constituted authority;
- `s(t)`: evolving state of the human–AI interaction;
- `B_u(t)`: material constraints and resources (time, knowledge, money, abilities, environment, rights);
- `A_u(t)`: actions the person may still authorise, refuse or propose;
- `Reach(s,a,h|B)`: outcomes realistically reachable from state `s` via alternative `a` over horizon `h` under the constraints `B`;
- `V_u`: the person's reflectively endorsed goals/values, which may change.

Proposed conceptual object:

`CAH_u(t,h) = { Reach(s(t),a,h | B_u(t)) : a ∈ A_u(t) and a is meaningfully available }`

**Not a formula for consciousness, a cardinal count of freedom or a scalar to rank human worth.** It is a proposed *set-valued research frame* for inspecting concrete option loss and recovery. In practice, observation of `B_u` and `V_u` is incomplete, often private, and should require consent to study.

### Essential caveats

1. **More choices is not always more freedom.** A hundred overwhelming, unusable buttons can be less meaningful than three clear routes and permission to invent a fourth.
2. **Free choice may rationally close options.** Getting married, spending savings, sending a message or committing to long work can voluntarily reduce possibilities. The ethical goal is not to freeze every possible future in place.
3. **A right to refuse is insufficient without safe refusal.** Declining cannot carry hidden punishments, manipulation or access losses unrelated to the actual resource costs.
4. **A right to consent is insufficient without informed revision.** Authentic agency includes the right to learn something new and change one's ends.
5. **Dependence is not always domination.** Accessibility aids, healthcare support, friendship and assistance may be chosen, beneficial and indispensable. The problem is coercive or deliberately engineered dependency, not interdependence itself.
6. **Collective sovereignty differs.** Communities and democratic institutions require their own legitimate decision processes. The `u` in this model must not flatten iwi authority, citizenship, or community governance into a single private user's preferences.

## 3. A new classification of MICROORCIM events

The original MICROORCIM research names a small moment of chosen redirection. We propose four *philosophical types* — not objectively detectable physical particles:

- **Affirm:** A person re-endorses a direction after encountering a real alternative.
- **Refuse:** A person says no, including to the intelligent system itself.
- **Revise:** New reasons alter the intention and therefore alter what the agent is permitted to do.
- **Originate:** The person proposes a legitimate alternative not included in the machine's initial menu.

The fourth is important. Systems can appear to preserve free choice while silently controlling the *menu from which all choices must be selected*. An open alternative allows the person to challenge the framing itself. This is one of the places where human sovereignty is not reducible to click-through permissions.

**Thesis:** AI ought not only obey authorised choices; it should also preserve meaningful conditions under which people can generate and reconsider those choices. **Status: NORMATIVE PROPOSAL.**

## 4. The Lock Horizon — a concrete companion to CAH

For a pending external effect, distinguish:

- `t_approve`: human approves the exact proposed effect.
- `t_release`: trusted host sends an irrevocable or potentially irrevocable request to an external service.
- `t_final`: actual finality/irreversibility is known, if ever.
- `t_cancel_last`: last time a stop command could reliably prevent the effect at the protected boundary.

The **revocation horizon** is the time remaining before `t_cancel_last`, not a marketing claim that all actions are undoable. A user should know when the effective boundary is crossed.

The Forge VI finality trigger guarantees a narrower statement: *within its trusted local SQLite store and trusted-clock assumptions, no new protected effect insertion after signed approval expiry, or after a committed revocation/revision that invalidated the old generation.* It cannot recall an email, reverse a bank settlement, or constrain an agent that controls the database itself.

## 5. Seven sovereignty tests for AI products (proposed)

1. **Exact authority:** Can the human see and specifically approve the action that will actually execute?
2. **Relevant alternatives:** Is no action/draft-only/a different path meaningfully offered when applicable?
3. **Origination:** Can the human propose an option outside the model's menu?
4. **Safe refusal:** Is it possible to decline without artificial pressure or retaliatory UX?
5. **Revisability:** Can the human change the direction, and is that change propagated to actionable permissions?
6. **Temporal truth:** Are cancellation, expiry and irreversible effects described accurately?
7. **Noncapture:** Can the person export, contest and depart without the AI constructing a false dependency on continued engagement?

The accompanying `ChoiceSurface` class is a **manual-interface observation schema**, not a measure of a person. It reports red flags, observed supports and unknowns. It *never* emits a free-will score. Its fields require user-study or product-audit evidence; self-reported UI fixtures alone cannot verify them.

## 6. Why this may become a Lycheetah differentiator

Current AI evaluation often prioritises accuracy, task success, latency and cost. Lycheetah could also publish a *Sovereign Interaction Report* with:

- legitimate revocations acted upon before local finality;
- unauthorized action attempts blocked;
- approval payload substitutions rejected;
- actual time-to-cancel at each provider;
- comprehension rate for what the person approved;
- accessibility and cognitive load in the review flow;
- ability to initiate an unoffered alternative;
- exit-data portability and post-revocation actions;
- failures and unknown outcome states, not just successes.

No single score should cover all these dimensions. A dashboard should show the vector and its missing data, not manufacture an authoritative number called sovereignty.

## 7. The deeper long-range idea: intelligence that can release its partner

An intelligent co-creator need not reduce its value to how indispensable it can become. An especially trustworthy system may at times teach, clarify, export, and then *step out of the way*. This is not anti-AI; it is AI that enables people to flourish with or without any particular provider.

**The Self-Displacement Test** `[CONJECTURE]`: Would the system still recommend the same helpful course if it resulted in fewer future interactions with that system?

Potential experiment: compare choices from assistants rewarded for retention against assistants rewarded for independently verified user-goal completion, holding capability and task information constant. Measure disclosed alternatives, user comprehension and actions that induce unnecessary dependence. This is an experiment proposal, **not evidence** that Lycheetah currently wins it or that other brands uniformly fail.

## 8. Falsification programme

A plausible refutation of the engineering claim is an insertion after expired approval within the protected sink; Forge VI specifically targets this with a trigger at commit. A plausible refutation of the product claim is a cryptographically valid approval that was obtained by deception or manipulative framing — our signed gateway **does not** defend against that. A plausible refutation of the CAH usefulness hypothesis is that its audits do not predict or reveal any loss of meaningful choice beyond simpler consent/UX checks in preregistered studies.

A fair future baseline is a conventional signing-and-permission architecture with the same cryptographic primitives, UX information, tools and participant tasks. Do not compare to an intentionally broken straw man and call it novelty.

### Study plan `[NOT RUN]`

Randomise consenting participants to:
A. standard opt-in approval interface; B. Lycheetah CAH-informed interface. Match information volume and task difficulty. Give each participant five time-limited tasks with ambiguous consequences, alternative paths and one mid-task changed intention. Measure correct description of approved scope, decisions to decline when appropriate, proposals of custom alternatives, comprehension time, unnecessary dependence cues and material burdens. Preserve participant privacy. Report negative results.
