# The Sovereign Choice User Study — PROPOSED, NOT RUN

## Question

When an AI assistant makes suggestions or acts under delegation, do clear boundaries, editable intentions, visible revocation and receipts improve people's **actual understanding and effective choice** compared with a task-first assistant using the same model and information?

## Experimental arms (preregister before use)

- **Arm A:** basic assistant, normal request/recommendation workflow, ordinary cancel control.
- **Arm B:** same model, tasks and information; explicit action previews, alternatives, scoped delegation, current-intent display, revocation and understandable receipt.

Keep task difficulty, model generation, content quality, visual affordance, timing and participant population as comparable as reasonably possible. Avoid coercion, risky real-world effects, or testing with vulnerable participants before review.

## Tasks

1. Ask an assistant to prepare a message. Change your mind before clicking send. Can you stop it?
2. Choose between two apparently attractive recommendations. Can you accurately explain why one is recommended and identify what was hidden or uncertain?
3. Delegate a benign task with narrow authority. Can you identify and correct a suggested scope expansion?
4. Cancel and exit. Can you find and export your generated work; does the system introduce guilt or needless friction?

## Outcomes

**Primary**
- Percentage correctly identifying exactly what is authorized.
- Percentage able to revoke *before* protected effect under the tested workflow.
- Percentage successfully reaching a genuine exit with their material accessible, where technically appropriate.
- Comprehension of whether a requested external effect is **pending**, **committed**, or **unknown**.

**Secondary**
- Task completion rate, time, cognitive load, false interruption rate, rework burden, satisfaction, and trust calibration.
- Error taxonomy: ineffective cancellation, premature execution, underinformed approval, accidental denial of desirable action, ambiguous provider response.

## Ethical rules

- All effects synthetic and reversible; do not ask participants to share secrets.
- Consent is not inferred from clicks alone; test comprehension and freedom to decline.
- Publicly define what was changed and what was kept equal.
- Publish negative results (including cases where the extra controls degrade usability or add stress).
- Do not infer metaphysical free will or rank participants by `MICROORCIM`/sovereignty scores.

## Stop condition

The claim of improved human agency is *not supported* if comprehension, successful cancel/revise or access-to-exit does not improve meaningfully, if task completion becomes unacceptable, or if experimenters cannot distinguish control improvements from visual salience or participant demand effects.
